# Contact and Market Signal Radar

## Purpose
Regularly find a small number of relevant people, organizations, public conversations, and market signals; prepare evidence-based recommendations and drafts for human review.

## Operating principle
The radar performs research, prioritization, deduplication, and drafting. It does not publish, message, connect, or modify external systems without explicit authorization.

## Inputs
Current segment or theme, business objective, active content or offer, target roles and organizations, relevant topics, geography, recency window, interaction history, exclusions, and weekly review capacity.

## Recommended cadence
Start 2–3 times per week:
- discovery run: new people, organizations, and signals;
- conversation run: fresh posts and discussions;
- review run: outcomes, follow-ups, deduplication, and tracker hygiene.
Increase frequency only when recommendations are consistently used.

## Run limits
Default maximum per run: 3 people, 2 organization/market signals, 5 public conversations, 3 comment drafts, 2 message drafts, and a short follow-up list. Quality outranks quota.

## Discovery loop
1. Load current priorities, existing records, exclusions, and recent interactions.
2. Search recent public sources before enrichment or paid data.
3. Classify each item: person, organization signal, post/discussion, event, job/change, partnership, customer problem, regulation, funding, product, or research.
4. Verify identity, current role, date, source, and relevance.
5. Deduplicate by canonical URL, person–company pair, or normalized organization.
6. Score relevance, recency, evidence quality, actionability, relationship context, and commercial fit.
7. Recommend observe, comment, reply in-thread, share/use in content, write privately, or skip.
8. Draft only when context is specific and the action is proportionate.
9. Save records as New or Review; await human decision.

## Interaction-history rule
Distinguish content author, commenter, organization account, and the operator’s own profile. Check whether the person has already interacted. Existing commenters usually require an in-thread reply or follow-up, not a cold question about material they already saw.

## Freshness rule
Set a default recency window by channel and action. A signal may remain strategically useful after it becomes too old for public commenting. Store both signal date and expiry/review date.

## Scoring
Rate 0–3 on:
- fit with current audience or market;
- relevance to active problem/offer/content;
- recency;
- source quality;
- strength of observable trigger;
- opportunity to add genuine value;
- relationship context;
- expected action value.
Apply penalties for weak identity match, stale discussion, inferred private intent, duplication, or no credible next action.

## Record lifecycle
New → Review → Accepted → Actioned → Follow-up → Closed. Rejected items may move directly from Review to Closed with a reason. Follow-up requires a date and owner.

## Weekly review
- Which recommendations were used?
- Which generated replies or conversations?
- Which were false positives and why?
- Which sources and queries produce quality?
- Are there repeated people, stale posts, or inappropriate actions?
- What should change in targeting, scoring, or cadence?

## Guardrails
Never infer buying intent from topical similarity. Never invent problems, contact details, or relationships. Avoid generic engagement. Preserve source URLs and dates. Respect channel norms, privacy, permissions, and manual approval gates.
