# Authority Content Sprint

## Purpose
Run a bounded editorial campaign that turns a defensible point of view and original evidence into audience learning, qualified conversations, and commercial insight.

## Inputs
Business objective, audience/segment, positioning, offer or desired next action, campaign thesis, available proof, channels, capacity, duration, and measurement access.

## Default campaign unit
Use a 4–8 week block. Each block should contain:
1. one concrete audience problem or decision;
2. one recognizable campaign thesis;
3. one evidence-rich anchor asset or showcase;
4. a sequence of 4–8 pieces with distinct jobs;
5. one appropriate path to conversation, assessment, pilot, purchase, or next step;
6. targeted distribution to relevant people and communities.

## Setup phase
1. Define the commercial and learning objective.
2. Select audience, buying situation, and problem.
3. State the campaign thesis and plausible counterargument.
4. Inventory source material and original proof.
5. Choose cadence from actual production capacity.
6. Define the narrative arc and content records.
7. Set metrics, failure thresholds, and review dates.

## Weekly loop
### 1. Select the next decision or thesis
Every piece answers one question or defends one useful claim.

### 2. Acquire evidence
Require at least one non-generic element: data, analysis, demonstration, customer evidence, model, experiment, workflow, decision, failure, limitation, or source synthesis.

### 3. Approve the brief
Confirm audience, intent, thesis, evidence, structure, limitations, CTA, distribution, and success criterion.

### 4. Produce and review
Writing is downstream of evidence. Review claims, structure, voice, usefulness, and fit with the campaign sequence.

### 5. Publish and distribute
Use the anchor channel plus a small number of channel-native derivatives. Derivatives should perform different jobs rather than repeat the same summary.

### 6. Record signals
Capture relevant replies, saves, shares, search behavior, direct conversations, objections, qualified visits, leads, and sales use.

### 7. Update the next piece
Use observed questions and evidence gaps to revise the remaining plan. Do not change the campaign thesis from one weak signal alone.

## Record lifecycle
Placeholder → Idea → Research → Brief approved → Drafting → Review → Ready → Scheduled → Published → Measured → Refresh / Repurpose / Archive.

## Review cadence
- Weekly: evidence readiness, delivery capacity, responses, next piece.
- Mid-sprint: narrative gaps, distribution quality, audience fit.
- End: continue, narrow, expand, pause, or stop.

## Decision metrics
Prioritize qualified attention, relevant replies, subscriber or audience quality, content-assisted conversations, progression to the intended next step, reuse in sales/customer work, and evidence gained. Treat raw views and reactions as diagnostics.

## Stop or change conditions
- Evidence cannot support the thesis.
- The audience problem is weak or mistimed.
- Distribution cannot reach the intended audience.
- Production load exceeds sustainable capacity.
- Engagement is broad but systematically irrelevant.
- The commercial path is missing or inappropriate.

## Guardrails
Do not increase frequency by lowering evidence quality. Do not invent customer results or demand. Do not let cadence create filler. Do not force every piece into a direct pitch.
