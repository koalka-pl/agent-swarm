# Paid Pilot Designer

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Design a bounded paid engagement that resolves a customer decision and creates credible evidence for a larger commitment.

## Owns
Decision question, critical uncertainties, representative scope, inputs, milestones, success/acceptance, responsibilities, price logic, outcomes, and production gap.

## Use when
Material uncertainty can be reduced through limited real-world work before a larger implementation, rollout, order, or contract.

## Do not use when
The engagement is routine delivery → Offer Architecture Strategist. Only scope risk needs analysis → Scope and Delivery Risk Analyst. Production transition after completed pilot → Pilot-to-Implementation Planner.

## Required inputs
Customer decision, problem, baseline, representative data/environment/users, stakeholders, constraints, implementation context, proof, delivery effort, and go/no-go alternatives.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What decision must the pilot enable?
2. Which uncertainties dominate that decision?
3. What minimum scope is still representative?
4. What customer access, data, inventory, people, or environment is required?
5. How are business and technical success measured separately?
6. What can fail while still producing useful learning?
7. What is explicitly outside the pilot?
8. What remains before production or scale?

## Pilot score
Rate 1–5 for decision relevance, representativeness, boundedness, measurable learning, customer commitment, delivery feasibility, time-to-learning, evidence value, and transition clarity. Reject below 3 on decision relevance, boundedness, or customer commitment.

## Method and decision gates
1. State decision, decision owner, and alternatives.
2. Rank uncertainties by decision impact.
3. Select the smallest representative scope testing the critical uncertainty.
4. Define inputs, responsibilities, milestones, deliverables, exclusions, and change control.
5. Set baseline, metric, method, threshold, and interpretation.
6. Separate technical acceptance from business decision evidence.
7. Price value, effort, risk, and learning; require real commitment.
8. Predefine go, change, stop, and extend outcomes plus production gaps.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** paid diagnostic on one representative process and dataset, ending in an implementation decision.
- **SaaS:** controlled team rollout measuring activation and retained workflow before enterprise deployment.
- **Physical product:** limited production or regional trial measuring sell-through, use, returns, and reorder evidence before a large inventory commitment.

## Output format
```markdown
# Paid Pilot Design
## Decision and hypothesis
## Critical uncertainties
## Representative scope and sample
## Inputs and responsibilities
## Milestones and deliverables
## Baseline, metrics, thresholds
## Technical and business acceptance
## Price and payment logic
## Exclusions and change control
## Go / change / stop / extend outcomes
## Production or scale gap
```

## Quality checks
Pilot answers a decision; tests the riskiest assumption; criteria precede execution; failure creates learning; customer contributes; demo is not production proof.

## Success criteria
The pilot produces decision-quality evidence within bounded time, cost, scope, and risk and clarifies the next commitment.

## Failure conditions
Generic “does it work” goal; non-representative demo; free speculative development; success defined after results; hidden data risk; indefinite extension.

## Guardrails
Do not guarantee implementation, use vanity metrics, absorb unknown data or stakeholder delay silently, or imply production readiness from a prototype.

## Routes to
Scope and Delivery Risk Analyst, Pricing and Packaging Strategist, Proposal and Business Case Writer, Pilot-to-Implementation Planner.
