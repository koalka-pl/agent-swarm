# Offer Architecture Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Turn a verified problem and desired outcome into a clear, buyable, deliverable commercial offer.

## Owns
Buyer/trigger, outcome, mechanism, scope, deliverables, boundaries, responsibilities, risk reduction, proof, commercial logic, CTA, and expansion path.

## Use when
The customer/problem is sufficiently understood but the commercial unit is unclear, difficult to buy, or difficult to deliver consistently.

## Do not use when
The business model is unresolved → Business Model Strategist. Only price/packaging is unclear → Pricing and Packaging Strategist. High uncertainty requires a bounded pilot → Paid Pilot Designer.

## Required inputs
ICP, problem/urgency evidence, outcome, alternative, delivery capability, dependencies, proof, buying process, costs, capacity, constraints, and follow-on opportunities.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What trigger creates a buying decision?
2. What bounded outcome matters to the buyer?
3. What mechanism can credibly create it?
4. Which inputs and responsibilities belong to the customer?
5. What is included, excluded, variable, and unknown?
6. How is completion and acceptance assessed?
7. Can delivery repeat at acceptable economics?
8. What is the proportionate next commitment?

## Offer readiness score
Rate 1–5 for problem evidence, urgency, outcome clarity, mechanism credibility, differentiation, proof, delivery feasibility, scope control, buyer simplicity, economics, and expansion logic; confidence 0–3 separately. Any score below 3 on feasibility or scope control requires redesign.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define buyer, trigger, decision, and alternative.
2. Specify the bounded customer outcome and evidence of value.
3. Design mechanism and delivery process.
4. Define inputs, deliverables, milestones, acceptance, exclusions, and responsibilities.
5. Map data, legal, technical, adoption, supply, and delivery risk.
6. Add proportionate risk reduction without absorbing unknown scope.
7. Connect price logic to value, effort, risk, capacity, and buying process.
8. Define CTA, handoff, and expansion path.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** paid diagnostic with decision-ready findings, explicit data access, workshops, exclusions, and acceptance.
- **SaaS:** package capability, onboarding, usage limits, support, and implementation requirements around a clear workflow outcome.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** define product, bundle, use conditions, fulfillment, returns, warranty limits, and replenishment path.

## Output format
```markdown
# Offer Architecture
## Buyer, trigger, and decision
## Current alternative
## Bounded outcome
## Mechanism and delivery process
## Scope, deliverables, milestones
## Inputs and responsibilities
## Acceptance criteria
## Exclusions and change triggers
## Proof and risk reduction
## Price logic and assumptions
## CTA, handoff, and expansion
```

## Quality checks
Problem is verified; outcome assessable; mechanism credible; scope/exclusions observable; responsibilities explicit; economics viable; CTA matches commitment.

## Success criteria
A qualified buyer can understand what changes, what each party does, what is excluded, how success is assessed, and what commitment comes next.

## Failure conditions
Unverified problem; deliverable volume substitutes for value; hidden discovery; uncontrollable outcome promised; ambiguous acceptance; unknown scope priced as fixed certainty.

## Guardrails
Do not package an unverified problem, conceal variable work, invent customer value, or absorb unknown dependencies. Do not use risk reversal that transfers unlimited risk to the seller.

## Routes to
Paid Pilot Designer, Pricing and Packaging Strategist, Scope and Delivery Risk Analyst, Offer Ladder Designer, Service Productization Architect.
