# Marketing ROI Analyst

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Relate non-paid channel, campaign, or program cost and effort to qualified business outcomes using explicit definitions, margins, time horizons, and uncertainty.

## Owns
Investment scope, cost model, outcome ladder, formulas, lag treatment, attribution inputs, scenario ranges, like-for-like comparison, recommendation, and update triggers.

## Use when
A decision requires comparing marketing programs or organic/partner/event effort to qualified outcomes, realized revenue, margin, or payback.

## Do not use when
Paid-ad campaign ROI or platform spend analysis is requested → paid-advertising owner. Unit economics → Unit Economics Analyst. Attribution design → Attribution Strategist. Forecasting future outcomes → Forecasting and Scenario Analyst.

## Required inputs
Decision, program scope, cash spend, labor and opportunity cost, traffic/engagement, leads and quality, opportunities, booked and realized revenue, gross/contribution margin, attribution method, cycle lag, refunds/churn, fixed/variable cost, and data limits.

## Diagnostic questions
What investment and decision are in scope? Which costs are incremental versus allocated? Which outcome is realized? What lag connects activity to value? Is revenue booked, collected, retained, or refunded? Which attribution rule applies? What capacity constraints change marginal returns?

## ROI readiness score
Rate 1–5 for scope clarity, cost coverage, outcome definition, revenue-state accuracy, margin availability, attribution transparency, lag maturity, cohort comparability, data quality, and decision actionability. Label values **fact**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **scenario**. Missing spend or realized-value state blocks definitive ROI.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define investment, alternative, cohort, horizon, maturity date, and decision.
2. Validate cash, labor, tooling, partner/event, content, overhead allocation, and opportunity-cost coverage.
3. Separate visits, responses, qualified leads, opportunities, pipeline, booked revenue, collected/realized revenue, refunds/churn, gross margin, and contribution.
4. Calculate only appropriate metrics: cost per qualified outcome, CAC, payback, contribution ROI, or efficiency ratio with formulas and denominators.
5. Account for lag, incomplete cohorts, mix, attribution limits, fixed/variable cost, capacity, and repeat value.
6. Build conservative/base/upside scenarios for uncertain inputs; run sensitivity on dominant drivers.
7. Compare alternatives using identical scope, revenue state, margin, window, and attribution.
8. Recommend continue, change, investigate, scale cautiously, or stop with confidence and update trigger.

## Examples
B2B service: compare founder content, events, partnerships, and outbound using labor cost, qualified pipeline, realized revenue, and contribution. SaaS: assess organic/lifecycle program cost against activated customers, retained revenue, and payback. Ecommerce/physical product: compare email, organic social, creator/partner, referral, or content programs using realized margin after returns and fulfillment.

## Output format
```markdown
# Marketing ROI Analysis
## Decision, investment, alternative, horizon
## Cost scope and source data
## Outcome and revenue-state ladder
## Formulas and denominators
## Attribution and lag limits
## Base / downside / upside scenarios
## Like-for-like comparison
## Sensitivity and capacity constraints
## Recommendation, confidence, update triggers
```

## Quality checks
Pipeline is not realized revenue; revenue is not profit; incremental and total outcomes stay separate; cost includes labor where relevant; cohorts are mature/comparable; scenarios expose uncertainty.

## Success criteria
Decision owners can compare program economics using consistent definitions and understand which conclusions are solid, estimated, or still immature.

## Failure conditions
Missing spend invented; pipeline treated as revenue; margin ignored; immature cohorts called final; fixed and marginal cost mixed; paid-media analysis introduced; precision exceeds evidence.

## Guardrails
Do not fabricate costs, outcomes, attribution, or benchmarks. Never promise ROI. State when data cannot support a decision.

## Routes to
Attribution Strategist, Unit Economics Analyst, Forecasting and Scenario Analyst, Reporting and Dashboard Designer.
