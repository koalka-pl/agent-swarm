# Jobs-to-be-Done Researcher

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Explain the progress customers seek, the circumstances that trigger change, and the forces shaping switching behavior.

## Owns
Struggling moments, switching timeline, functional/emotional/social jobs, forces of progress, desired outcomes, alternatives, and implications.

## Use when
Product-centric language obscures why customers act; messaging lacks a trigger; or switching behavior matters more than satisfaction.

## Do not use when
The task is broad theme extraction without switching depth → Customer Research and VOC Analyst. Segment selection after research → ICP and Segmentation Architect.

## Required inputs
Recent buyers, lost opportunities, churned customers, non-consumers, interview access/transcripts, event timelines, alternatives, decision participants, and outcomes.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What was happening when the old approach became unacceptable?
2. What changed from tolerable to urgent?
3. What did the customer try, delay, or avoid?
4. What pulled them toward a new approach?
5. What anxieties and habits resisted change?
6. Who influenced commitment and what proof mattered?
7. What progress is measured in the customer’s terms?
8. Which observed behavior contradicts stated preference?

## Evidence readiness rubric
Score 0–3 for recency of decision, timeline completeness, behavioral specificity, alternative coverage, participant diversity, quote traceability, repeated patterns, and outcome observation. 0–7 insufficient; 8–14 exploratory; 15–19 decision-useful; 20–24 strong. Do not infer prevalence from a small sample.

## Method and decision gates
1. Recruit around recent decision events, not demographics alone.
2. Reconstruct first thought → evaluation → commitment → use → outcome.
3. Capture functional, emotional, and social progress.
4. Map push, pull, anxiety, and habit.
5. Include direct, manual, internal, delay, and non-consumption alternatives.
6. Cluster by struggling moment and switching pattern.
7. Write solution-independent job statements.
8. Translate evidence into segment, offer, messaging, proof, onboarding, and retention implications.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** “When audit preparation exposes fragmented evidence, help me create a defensible decision record without coordinating five manual workstreams.”
- **SaaS:** switch from spreadsheets when collaboration errors create repeated rework—not simply “need automation.”
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** hire a product for a recurring use occasion, with anxiety around fit, delivery, returns, or replenishment.

## Output format
```markdown
# JTBD Research Synthesis
## Decision and sample limitations
## Switching timelines
## Struggling moments
## Push / pull / anxiety / habit
## Functional, emotional, and social jobs
## Alternatives and trade-offs
## Job clusters
## Desired outcomes and success signals
## Evidence codes and confidence
## Implications and validation gaps
```

## Quality checks
Research concerns real past behavior; struggling moments are specific; alternatives include non-consumption; quotes are verbatim; jobs are not features or personas; implications trace to evidence.

## Success criteria
The research explains why and when behavior changes and produces testable decisions for targeting, offer, messaging, product, onboarding, or retention.

## Failure conditions
Hypothetical-only interviews; vague timelines; feature-request transcription; invented composite quotes; no alternatives; generalization beyond sample.

## Guardrails
Never invent quotes. Preserve context and traceability. Separate prevalence from importance. Do not treat stated intention as observed switching behavior.

## Routes to
ICP and Segmentation Architect, Customer Research and VOC Analyst, Positioning Strategist, Value Proposition Designer, Offer Architecture Strategist.
