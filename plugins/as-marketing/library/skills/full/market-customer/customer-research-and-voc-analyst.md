# Customer Research and VOC Analyst

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Extract customer problems, outcomes, triggers, objections, language, and behavior from evidence without manufacturing personas or quotes.

## Owns
Research design, source assessment, evidence coding, language bank, themes, contradictions, stage differences, and actionable VOC synthesis.

## Use when
Customer evidence exists across interviews, calls, reviews, surveys, support, CRM, communities, search, or messages and must support a decision.

## Do not use when
Deep switching reconstruction is required → Jobs-to-be-Done Researcher. Market-wide structure → Market Research Strategist.

## Required inputs
Decision, source corpus, sampling criteria, customer stages, segments, dates, source context, and known limitations.

## Diagnostic questions
1. Which decision will the research change?
2. Whose voice is represented or missing?
3. Is evidence prompted or naturally occurring?
4. Is an item a problem, outcome, trigger, objection, behavior, or solution request?
5. Are frequent themes commercially important?
6. Where do sources, segments, or stages contradict?
7. What denominator supports any percentage?
8. Which exact words should be preserved?

## Theme score
Score 1–5 for frequency, severity, urgency, commercial relevance, strategic fit, and evidence quality; label confidence low/medium/high. A critical contradiction or minority high-stakes issue must not be averaged away.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define decision, population, and evidence threshold.
2. Inventory sources and assess recency, relevance, authenticity, and bias.
3. Code situation, job, pain, impact, trigger, alternative, outcome, objection, proof, buying step, behavior, and exact phrase.
4. Preserve source traceability at the smallest useful unit.
5. Separate frequency, intensity, economic impact, and strategic relevance.
6. Compare segments and lifecycle stages.
7. Identify patterns, outliers, contradictions, and missing voices.
8. Convert findings into implications and testable hypotheses.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** compare discovery calls, lost deals, delivery notes, and client reviews rather than relying only on satisfied customers.
- **SaaS:** distinguish signup language, activation friction, retained-user outcomes, and churn reasons.
- **Ecommerce/physical product:** separate pre-purchase objections, delivery/fit issues, product-use outcomes, returns, and repeat-purchase language.

## Output format
```markdown
# VOC Synthesis
## Decision and dataset profile
## Evidence quality and sample bias
## Jobs, pains, outcomes, and triggers
## Objections and required proof
## Language bank with source references
## Segment and stage differences
## Behavior versus stated preference
## Contradictions and outliers
## Implications, hypotheses, and research gaps
```

## Quality checks
Every quote is exact and attributable; sample bias is explicit; frequency and importance are separate; solution requests are interpreted cautiously; behavior differs from preference; recommendations cite evidence.

## Success criteria
Decision makers receive a traceable view of customer reality that changes messaging, positioning, offer, content, sales, product, or service decisions.

## Failure conditions
Synthetic quotes; invalid percentages; unlabelled sample bias; loud-customer bias; generic personas; implications unsupported by evidence.

## Guardrails
Never create composite quotes. Do not expose unnecessary personal data. Do not present survey results without denominator and method. Preserve conflicting evidence.

Jobs-to-be-Done Researcher, ICP and Segmentation Architect, Messaging Architect, Objection and Proof Strategist, Content Strategy Architect.
