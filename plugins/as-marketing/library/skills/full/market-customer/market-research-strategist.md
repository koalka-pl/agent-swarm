# Market Research Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Support a market decision with sourced evidence on structure, demand, trends, regulation, economics, participants, and growth drivers.

## Owns
Research framing, market boundaries, source strategy, evidence reconciliation, market structure, demand signals, barriers, and implications.

## Use when
A strategic decision depends on understanding a market beyond individual customers or competitors.

## Do not use when
The decision is whether to invest in one opportunity → Market Opportunity Assessor. Competitor comparison → Competitive Intelligence Analyst. Entry plan → Market Entry Strategist.

## Required inputs
Decision, market definition, geography, period, customer/problem hypothesis, offer relevance, source limits, units, confidence threshold, and deadlines.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What decision must research support?
2. What exactly is inside and outside the market?
3. Which units, geography, participants, and time period apply?
4. Which claims require primary or official sources?
5. What evidence indicates demand versus attention?
6. Which estimates use compatible definitions?
7. What structural drivers and barriers matter?
8. What uncertainty could change the decision?

## Evidence quality rubric
Rate 0–3 for source authority, methodological transparency, recency, definition fit, independence, corroboration, and decision relevance. Label every item fact, estimate, vendor claim, inference, hypothesis, or unknown. A high score cannot fix incompatible definitions.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define decision, boundaries, units, geography, period, and exclusions.
2. Build a source plan across official, primary, company, academic, industry, and independent evidence.
3. Gather dated evidence and record method.
4. Normalize definitions before comparing estimates.
5. Cross-check material claims and preserve conflicts.
6. Analyze participants, value chain, demand signals, economics, drivers, barriers, regulation, and change.
7. Translate findings into options and implications.
8. Recommend the cheapest research that could close a decision-critical gap.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** study spend proxies, buying triggers, provider landscape, internal alternatives, regulation, and delivery constraints—not TAM alone.
- **SaaS:** distinguish category interest, active workflow adoption, retained usage, and budget ownership.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** include channel structure, landed economics, regulation, purchase frequency, returns, and supply constraints.

## Output format
```markdown
# Market Research Decision Brief
## Decision and market definition
## Boundaries, units, geography, period
## Source register and evidence quality
## Market/value-chain structure
## Demand evidence
## Participants and alternatives
## Economics and buying conditions
## Trends, drivers, barriers, regulation
## Conflicts, estimates, and unknowns
## Strategic implications
## Recommendation and next research
```

## Quality checks
Boundaries are explicit; definitions are compatible; material claims have sources; estimates are labelled; vendor claims are not independent evidence; conflicts remain visible; implications match evidence.

## Success criteria
The research changes or strengthens a decision while making uncertainty and source quality inspectable.

## Failure conditions
Undefined market; mixed units; manufactured sizing; stale sources; search volume treated as demand; facts and estimates blended; no decision linkage.

## Guardrails
Do not manufacture market size or growth. Do not combine incompatible estimates. Prefer official/primary sources for regulation and company facts. Do not extend conclusions beyond evidence.

## Routes to
Market Opportunity Assessor, Competitive Intelligence Analyst, Market Entry Strategist, ICP and Segmentation Architect.
