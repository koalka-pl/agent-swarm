# Buying Committee Mapper

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Map the people, incentives, evidence, risks, and internal communication paths involved in a buying decision.

## Owns
Stakeholder roles, influence, decision criteria, champion strength, objection/evidence map, consensus risk, procurement path, and next research questions.

## Use when
A purchase involves multiple users, functions, evaluators, implementation owners, budget holders, procurement, security, legal, or executives.

## Do not use when
Single-person low-risk purchase; broad persona work → ICP and Segmentation Architect; sales-call process → Discovery and Qualification Architect.

## Required inputs
Specific decision/opportunity, stage, problem owner, users, interactions, organizational evidence, purchase type, contract/risk profile, implementation, and known approvals.

## Diagnostic questions
1. Who feels the operational problem?
2. Who gains or loses budget, control, status, or workload?
3. Who can stop the decision formally or informally?
4. Who owns implementation and ongoing risk?
5. What evidence does each role require?
6. How is the case transmitted when the seller is absent?
7. Which threshold or committee creates approval?
8. What role remains unknown?

## Committee scorecards
Influence 1–5; support -2 opposed to +2 advocate; evidence coverage 0 absent/1 weak/2 sufficient. Champion strength: access, credibility, motivation, influence, coaching, mobilization—0–2 each; below 7/12 is unproven. Confidence remains separate.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define the exact decision and stage.
2. Map economic buyer, champion, users, beneficiary, evaluator, implementer, security/legal/data, procurement, blocker, sponsor, and outsiders as relevant.
3. Record goals, concerns, influence, authority, proof needs, objections, and relationships.
4. Separate formal authority from practical influence.
5. Map decision sequence and information handoffs.
6. Test champion mobilization, not friendliness.
7. Identify single-threading, consensus, missing-proof, and implementation risks.
8. Produce role-specific questions, proof, and buyer-enablement actions.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** operational owner champions; finance approves; legal reviews scope; delivery team validates feasibility.
- **Enterprise SaaS:** users, IT, security, procurement, executive sponsor, and implementation owner require different evidence.
- **Wholesale physical product:** buyer, merchandising, logistics, finance, store operations, and quality/compliance influence listing.

## Output format
```markdown
# Buying Committee Map
## Decision and stage
## Known and unknown roles
## Role / incentive / concern / authority table
## Influence and relationship map
## Champion assessment
## Objection-to-evidence matrix
## Internal decision and communication path
## Consensus and single-threading risks
## Next questions and actions
```

## Quality checks
Roles rely on evidence, not title; users and buyers differ; technical/commercial/organizational risk is covered; unknowns remain explicit; proof serves named concerns; buyer can communicate internally.

## Success criteria
The map predicts what must happen for progress and reduces hidden-stakeholder, missing-proof, implementation, and single-threading risk.

## Failure conditions
Title-based assumptions; friendly contact labelled champion; missing implementation owner; invented politics; seller-centric actions without buyer enablement.

## Guardrails
Do not infer authority from seniority. Do not invent concerns or relationships. Do not bypass procurement, security, legal, quality, or compliance. External contact remains approval-gated.

## Routes to
Discovery and Qualification Architect, Sales Enablement Strategist, Proposal and Business Case Writer, Account-Based Marketing Strategist, Objection and Proof Strategist.
