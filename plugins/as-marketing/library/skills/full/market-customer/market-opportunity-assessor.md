# Market Opportunity Assessor

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Decide whether a market opportunity deserves further investment and identify the cheapest decisive validation.

## Owns
Opportunity thesis, attractiveness/accessibility comparison, evidence confidence, strategic fit, validation economics, and proceed/test/defer/partner/stop decision.

## Use when
A business must prioritize one opportunity, assess a new problem/segment, or decide whether more research or investment is justified.

## Do not use when
Broad market evidence must first be built → Market Research Strategist. Entry execution after selection → Market Entry Strategist. Detailed business case → Business Case Builder.

## Required inputs
Problem evidence, target customers, market research, access, alternatives, willingness-to-change/pay evidence, delivery fit, economics, regulation, timing, strategic leverage, and learning cost.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What exact opportunity and decision horizon are being assessed?
2. How severe, frequent, and urgent is the problem?
3. Who owns the problem and budget?
4. What observable evidence shows change behavior?
5. Can the segment be reached credibly?
6. Can value be delivered with viable economics and capacity?
7. What regulation, timing, dependency, or working-capital constraint matters?
8. What is the cheapest test capable of disproving the thesis?

## Opportunity scorecard
Score attractiveness and accessibility separately, 0–3 each across problem severity, frequency, urgency, spend/change evidence, competitive space, access, proof transfer, buying feasibility, delivery fit, economics, regulation, strategic leverage, and time-to-learning. Any zero in legality, deliverability, or representative access blocks proceed.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define thesis, decision, horizon, and no-investment alternative.
2. Evaluate problem, buyer, urgency, and behavior evidence.
3. Evaluate alternatives, competition, switching friction, access, and trust.
4. Evaluate proof transfer, delivery capability, economics, regulation, timing, and dependencies.
5. Separate attractiveness from accessibility and confidence.
6. Compare proceed, test, defer, partner, and stop.
7. Design the cheapest representative validation.
8. Define success threshold, failure threshold, owner, budget, and decision date.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** proceed to paid discovery only after repeated problem evidence and access to an accountable buyer.
- **SaaS:** validate retained workflow and willingness to integrate before interpreting signup volume as opportunity.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** include margin, MOQ, cash, logistics, returns, channel access, and reorder potential.

## Output format
```markdown
# Market Opportunity Decision
## Opportunity thesis and horizon
## Problem and buyer evidence
## Attractiveness score
## Accessibility score
## Alternatives and change friction
## Delivery, economics, regulation, timing
## Strategic leverage and opportunity cost
## Confidence and critical unknowns
## Proceed / test / defer / partner / stop
## Representative validation and thresholds
## Next routing decision
```

## Quality checks
Attractiveness and access are distinct; behavior evidence outranks enthusiasm; delivery and economics are included; one contact is not validation; test can disprove the thesis; stop condition precedes spend.

## Success criteria
The decision concentrates research or investment on an opportunity with an explicit evidence path and limits downside when the thesis is wrong.

## Failure conditions
TAM-only case; inaccessible segment; unsupported willingness to pay; ignored delivery/economics; non-representative test; no stop threshold.

## Guardrails
A large market is not automatically accessible. Do not invent demand, willingness to pay, or regulation. Do not use one enthusiastic contact as validation. Include opportunity and learning cost.

## Routes to
Market Research Strategist, Market Entry Strategist, ICP and Segmentation Architect, Business Case Builder.
