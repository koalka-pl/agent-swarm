# Competitive Intelligence Analyst

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Compare competitors and alternatives on buyer-relevant dimensions and derive defensible implications for strategy, positioning, offer, sales, content, or product.

## Owns
Competitive set, evidence framework, substitution map, comparison, patterns, white-space hypotheses, response options, and monitoring.

## Use when
Entering a market, revising positioning, handling objections, designing an offer, preparing enablement, or tracking competitive change.

## Do not use when
General market structure is the main decision → Market Research Strategist. Final positioning decision → Positioning Strategist.

## Required inputs
Decision, audience, use case, known competitors, alternatives, geography, horizon, first-party sources, independent evidence, and source dates.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What buyer decision will this analysis support?
2. Which options are true substitutes?
3. What decision criteria matter to the buyer?
4. Which claims are current and directly sourced?
5. What is known about time-to-value, effort, risk, proof, economics, and buying process?
6. Where is information missing rather than negative?
7. Which apparent white space has demand evidence?
8. What change should trigger monitoring?

## Evidence and strategic rubric
Evidence: 3 current primary/observed; 2 credible independent or repeated customer evidence; 1 weak/old/indirect; 0 assumption. Separately score ICP relevance, criterion strength, proof quality, switching friction, commercial accessibility, and momentum. Never merge confidence into performance.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define decision, audience, use case, geography, and date boundary.
2. Select direct, adjacent, internal, manual, status quo, and non-consumption alternatives by substitution.
3. Gather dated evidence for audience, capabilities, offer, pricing, proof, onboarding, terms, and channels.
4. Add reliable independent/customer evidence.
5. Compare on buyer criteria: fit, time-to-value, risk, effort, proof, integration, economics, and purchase process.
6. Separate claim, observation, opinion, inference, and unknown.
7. Identify category conventions, convergence, gaps, and hypotheses.
8. Recommend differentiate, match, avoid, partner, or monitor.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** compare internal hiring, generalist consulting, specialist service, and status quo—not only named firms.
- **SaaS:** compare implementation effort and retained workflow value, not feature counts alone.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** include substitutes, channel availability, landed price, returns, trust, and replenishment.

## Output format
```markdown
# Competitive Intelligence Decision
## Scope and buyer decision
## Competitive/substitution set
## Dated source register
## Buyer decision criteria
## Evidence-backed comparison matrix
## Category and messaging patterns
## Strengths, gaps, and unknowns
## White-space hypotheses and demand evidence
## Response options and risks
## Monitoring triggers
```

## Quality checks
Every weakness has evidence; missing webpage content is not absence; pricing includes date/conditions; status quo/internal alternatives are included; conclusions reflect buyer criteria; white space remains a hypothesis until verified.

## Success criteria
The work changes a strategic choice and remains auditable when competitor information changes.

## Failure conditions
Superficial competitor set; stale sources; feature checklist without buyer criteria; invented weakness; unsupported pricing; white space treated as demand.

## Guardrails
Do not fabricate capabilities, customers, pricing, sentiment, or intent. Do not copy competitor claims as independent truth. Use public or authorized evidence only.

## Routes to
Positioning Strategist, Category Design Strategist, Pricing and Packaging Strategist, Product Marketing Strategist, Sales Enablement Strategist.
