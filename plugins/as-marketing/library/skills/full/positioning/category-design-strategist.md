# Category Design Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Decide whether to compete in an existing category, define a subcategory, or introduce a new market frame.

## Owns
Category choice, problem frame, comparison set, vocabulary, education burden, proof needs, adoption risk, and validation.

## Use when
Existing category language misframes value, meaningful differentiation needs a clearer subcategory, or a genuinely new buying frame may be justified.

## Do not use when
Ordinary positioning within a useful category → Positioning Strategist. Generic naming exercise or campaign concept.

## Required inputs
Buyer language, categories, alternatives, unmet problem, behavior change, differentiation, proof, education capacity, acquisition economics, and market evidence.

## Diagnostic questions
1. How do buyers currently name the problem and shortlist options?
2. What useful comprehension does the existing category provide?
3. What valuable difference does it hide?
4. Is the problem frame genuinely distinct?
5. What behavior must buyers change?
6. What education and distribution capacity exists?
7. What proof makes the frame credible?
8. Can a subcategory achieve the goal at lower cost?

## Category option score
Rate 0–3 for comprehension, relevance, differentiation, credibility, existing demand, education burden, proof coverage, acquisition feasibility, and durability. New-category options require stronger evidence and capacity than subcategory options.

## Method and decision gates
1. Map current buyer language, categories, and substitutes.
2. Define the problem, point of view, alternative, and behavior change.
3. Compare existing category, subcategory, and new-category paths.
4. Estimate comprehension gain versus education cost.
5. Define vocabulary, category boundaries, proof, and exemplars.
6. Design education and distribution sequence.
7. Test recognition, relevance, shortlist behavior, and conversion quality.
8. Retreat to a simpler frame if education cost exceeds strategic value.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** create a narrow operational subcategory only if it changes buyer criteria and proof requirements.
- **SaaS:** avoid a new category when buyers already search and budget within a useful existing one.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** define a new use occasion only when customer behavior and channel education support it.

## Output format
```markdown
# Category Decision
## Buyer language and current categories
## Existing frame benefits/constraints
## Distinct problem and point of view
## Existing / subcategory / new-category comparison
## Recommended frame and vocabulary
## Boundaries and alternatives
## Education and proof plan
## Risks, economics, and tests
## Stop/reframe conditions
```

## Quality checks
Category solves a comprehension problem; behavior change is explicit; education burden is costed; demand is not assumed; vocabulary is usable; subcategory alternative is considered.

## Success criteria
The selected frame improves buyer understanding and strategic differentiation enough to justify its education and acquisition cost.

## Failure conditions
Category as naming stunt; no distinct problem; invented demand; high education burden without distribution; claim of ownership; no behavior test.

## Guardrails
Creating a category increases cost and risk. Do not claim category ownership, leadership, or demand without evidence. Prefer simpler frames when they work.

## Routes to
Positioning Strategist, Messaging Architect, Market Research Strategist, Content Strategy Architect.
