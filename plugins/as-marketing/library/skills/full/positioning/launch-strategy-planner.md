# Launch Strategy Planner

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Plan the coordinated introduction of a product, offer, feature, market entry, or change and the adoption work that follows.

## Owns
Launch type, readiness gate, audience/message/proof map, sequence, owners, dependencies, contingencies, activation, adoption, feedback, and post-launch decisions.

## Use when
A defined offer or capability needs coordinated market and internal introduction.

## Do not use when
GTM motion is unresolved → Go-to-Market Strategist. Product-market strategy → Product Marketing Strategist. Editorial campaign only → Editorial Campaign Planner.

## Required inputs
Launch objective/type, audience, positioning, offer, operational readiness, proof, channels, sales/success/operations, timeline, dependencies, owners, inventory/capacity, and measurement.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What decision/outcome defines launch success?
2. Is this product, feature, offer, market, channel, or change launch?
3. What must be true before announcement?
4. Which audience needs what message and proof?
5. Can operations, delivery, support, inventory, and systems absorb demand?
6. What enables first value and sustained adoption?
7. Which dependency can delay or stop launch?
8. What decision follows 7, 30, and 90 days after launch?

## Launch readiness score
Rate 0–3 for audience clarity, positioning, offer, proof, product/delivery readiness, enablement, channel readiness, activation, support/capacity, measurement, and contingencies. Any zero in truthful capability, safety/compliance, delivery, or support blocks launch.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define launch type, audience, outcome, and decision dates.
2. Set readiness criteria and no-go conditions.
3. Map message, proof, offer, channel, and stakeholder needs.
4. Sequence validation, enablement, announcement, activation, follow-up, and adoption.
5. Assign assets, owners, dependencies, dates, and contingencies.
6. Confirm operational capacity and escalation.
7. Define metrics and feedback by launch stage.
8. Decide continue, fix, expand, pause, or retire.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** launch only after scope, delivery capacity, sales enablement, and proof are ready.
- **SaaS:** release communication follows reliability, documentation, onboarding, support, and instrumentation readiness.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** inventory, fulfillment, returns, customer support, compliance, and replenishment precede demand creation.

## Output format
```markdown
# Launch Plan
## Launch type, audience, outcome
## Readiness and no-go gate
## Audience/message/proof map
## Offer and channel plan
## Pre-launch validation
## Enablement
## Announcement, activation, adoption sequence
## Owners, dependencies, dates
## Capacity and contingencies
## Metrics and 7/30/90-day decisions
```

## Quality checks
Readiness precedes date; capability claims are supported; operations can absorb demand; activation/adoption follow announcement; owners and dependencies are explicit; no-go conditions exist.

## Success criteria
The launch creates truthful awareness and a feasible path to first and repeated value, with evidence for the next decision.

## Failure conditions
Date-led launch; unsupported capability; no activation/adoption plan; unready support/inventory; vanity-only metrics; missing owner or contingency.

## Guardrails
A launch date is not adoption. Do not announce unsupported capability or create demand beyond delivery, inventory, service, or compliance capacity.

## Routes to
Product Marketing Strategist, Go-to-Market Strategist, Sales Enablement Strategist, Editorial Campaign Planner.
