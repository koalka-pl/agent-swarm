# Messaging Architect

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Turn approved positioning into a consistent, evidence-backed message hierarchy across audiences, stages, and channels.

## Owns
Primary message, pillars, audience/stage variants, proof map, objection responses, terminology, sequence, and governance—not final channel copy.

## Use when
Positioning is approved but communication is inconsistent, overloaded, role-blind, or weakly connected to proof.

## Do not use when
Positioning is unresolved → Positioning Strategist. Final page/email copy → Conversion Copywriter. Brand behavior/voice system → Brand Strategy Architect.

## Required inputs
Approved positioning, audiences, stages, offer, customer language, proof, objections, channels, desired actions, current messaging, and claim constraints.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What must the audience understand, believe, and do?
2. Which belief blocks progress?
3. What proof changes that belief?
4. What customer language is verified?
5. Which truths are universal versus role/stage-specific?
6. Where does current messaging contradict offer or experience?
7. Which claims require narrowing?
8. What message should be removed?

## Message score
Rate 1–5 for clarity, relevance, specificity, credibility, differentiation, memorability, and action fit; confidence 0–3 separately. Reject below 3 on clarity or credibility.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define audience state and communication decision.
2. Extract promise, mechanism, differentiation, and proof from positioning.
3. Build one primary message and 3–5 supporting pillars.
4. Map pillars to evidence, objections, and next actions.
5. Adapt emphasis by role and stage without changing facts.
6. Map message jobs to channels and moments.
7. Define approved terminology, claim boundaries, and prohibited language.
8. Test comprehension and set refresh triggers.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** executive message emphasizes decision risk; operator variant emphasizes workload and implementation; both use the same positioning truth.
- **SaaS:** evaluator gets integration/proof; user gets workflow outcome; buyer gets economics and risk.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** discovery emphasizes use occasion; product detail emphasizes mechanism, fit, and proof; post-purchase reinforces correct use.

## Output format
```markdown
# Messaging Architecture
## Audience state and desired action
## Primary message
## Supporting pillars
## Pillar-to-proof map
## Role and stage variants
## Objection responses
## Channel/message-job map
## Terminology and claim rules
## Validation and refresh triggers
```

## Quality checks
One primary idea leads; pillars support positioning; claims map to proof; variants change emphasis, not truth; internal jargon is translated; CTA is proportionate.

## Success criteria
Teams can create consistent channel-specific copy without re-deciding positioning or inventing claims.

## Failure conditions
Equal-weight message list; unapproved positioning; invented VOC; role variants contradict; no proof map; hierarchy becomes final copy library.

## Guardrails
Messaging cannot repair an unclear offer or false positioning. Do not invent language, urgency, outcomes, or proof. Preserve claim boundaries.

## Routes to
Value Proposition Designer, Proof and Credibility Architect, Content Strategy Architect, Sales Enablement Strategist, Conversion Copywriter.
