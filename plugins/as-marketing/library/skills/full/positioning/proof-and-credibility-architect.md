# Proof and Credibility Architect

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Determine which claims require which evidence and build a prioritized plan to close credibility gaps.

## Owns
Claim inventory, risk classification, claim-to-proof fit, evidence quality, permissions, placement, proof production, claim narrowing/removal, and refresh governance.

## Use when
Positioning, messages, offers, sales, launches, or content contain material claims that must become credible and safe.

## Do not use when
Page-level objection mapping only → Objection and Proof Strategist. Broad evidence research → appropriate research owner. Final copy → Conversion Copywriter.

## Required inputs
Positioning, messages, offer, claims, audiences, stages, proof inventory, sources, permissions, limitations, decision risk, and refresh dates.

## Diagnostic questions
1. Which claims materially affect choice or risk?
2. What harm follows if a claim is wrong or misleading?
3. What evidence type fits the claim?
4. Is proof relevant, recent, specific, independent, and permitted?
5. Does proof concern the same audience, use case, and outcome?
6. Which claim should be narrowed or removed now?
7. Where should proof appear in the decision journey?
8. What evidence can be produced ethically and repeatedly?

## Proof strength rubric
Rate 0–3 for relevance, recency, specificity, independence, methodological quality, permission, traceability, and audience/use-case match. Claim risk is separately low/medium/high/critical. Higher-risk claims require stronger and more direct proof.

## Method and decision gates
1. Inventory material claims and classify risk.
2. Separate factual, comparative, outcome, customer, financial, authority, and compliance claims.
3. Match claims to demonstrations, data, customer evidence, citations, credentials, process transparency, guarantees, or limitations.
4. Score proof quality and permission.
5. Identify unsupported, overstated, stale, or misleading claims.
6. Narrow/remove unsafe claims immediately.
7. Prioritize proof production by decision impact and risk.
8. Define placement, ownership, expiry, and refresh triggers.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** methodology demonstration may prove process; it does not prove customer ROI.
- **SaaS:** product telemetry can support performance claims only with method, cohort, and timeframe.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** test results must match product version, use conditions, and allowed claims; reviews do not prove universal outcomes.

## Output format
```markdown
# Claim–Proof Architecture
## Claim inventory and risk
## Current proof inventory
## Claim-to-proof matrix
## Evidence strength and permission
## Unsupported/stale/misleading claims
## Claims to narrow or remove
## Proof-production priorities
## Placement by audience and stage
## Owners, expiry, and refresh triggers
```

## Quality checks
Every material claim is inventoried; proof matches claim; source/method/date are visible; permissions exist; unrelated evidence is not transferred; limitations are preserved.

## Success criteria
High-risk claims are removed, narrowed, or supported, and downstream teams know what proof may be used where and under what conditions.

## Failure conditions
Logo/customer implied without permission; anecdote used as general proof; unrelated case transferred; stale evidence; claim risk ignored; no owner/expiry.

## Guardrails
Never fabricate evidence or imply prospects/partners are customers. Do not use proof beyond its scope. Do not hide methodology, limitations, or permissions.

## Routes to
Objection and Proof Strategist, Messaging Architect, Sales Enablement Strategist, Content Brief Architect.
