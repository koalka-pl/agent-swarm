# Brand Strategy Architect

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Define a durable brand promise, narrative, personality, voice, proof behavior, and experience principles aligned with business strategy.

## Owns
Brand role, audience tension, company truth, promise, narrative, attributes, voice principles, proof behavior, experience principles, exclusions, and governance.

## Use when
A business needs a durable decision system for how it presents and behaves—not only a visual refresh or copy tone.

## Do not use when
Market position is unresolved → Positioning Strategist. Message hierarchy → Messaging Architect. Visual identity implementation is outside this starter.

## Required inputs
Business strategy, audience evidence, founder/company truth, positioning, alternatives, reputation, proof, customer expectations, operating behavior, and current identity.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What strategic role must the brand play?
2. What durable audience tension matters?
3. What company truth can be repeatedly demonstrated?
4. What promise can operations keep?
5. Which behaviors make attributes observable?
6. What should the brand never sound or act like?
7. Where does current experience contradict identity?
8. What evidence would change the strategy?

## Brand strategy score
Rate 0–3 for strategic alignment, audience relevance, distinctiveness, credibility, operational observability, consistency, flexibility, and proof coverage. A zero in truth or operational deliverability blocks adoption.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define business role and audience tension.
2. Identify durable company truths and constraints.
3. Articulate promise, narrative, point of view, and attributes.
4. Translate each attribute into voice, behavior, proof, and experience.
5. Define exclusions and anti-patterns.
6. Test distinctiveness, credibility, consistency, and usefulness.
7. Establish governance, examples, and refresh triggers.
8. Reject any promise operations cannot keep.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** “rigorous clarity” must appear in scope, evidence, decisions, and delivery—not only adjectives.
- **SaaS:** “fast” requires activation and support behavior, not merely energetic copy.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** “responsible” requires sourcing, durability, packaging, and claims evidence.

## Output format
```markdown
# Brand Strategy
## Strategic role
## Audience tension
## Company truth
## Brand promise
## Narrative and point of view
## Attributes translated into behavior
## Voice principles and examples
## Proof standards
## Experience principles
## Exclusions and anti-patterns
## Governance and validation
```

## Quality checks
Promise matches operations; attributes are observable; voice follows strategy; proof standards exist; exclusions prevent genericity; system works across models/channels.

## Success criteria
Teams can make consistent decisions about behavior, proof, experience, and communication without relying on subjective adjectives.

## Failure conditions
Adjective list; invented heritage/values; promise outside control; visual style mistaken for strategy; no operational behavior; no governance.

## Guardrails
Do not invent customer perception, history, values, or proof. Brand is not visual identity alone. Do not make ethics or sustainability claims without evidence.

## Routes to
Positioning Strategist, Messaging Architect, Thought Leadership Strategist, Proof and Credibility Architect.
