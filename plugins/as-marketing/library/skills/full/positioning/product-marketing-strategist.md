# Product Marketing Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Connect capabilities to priority segments, use cases, competition, buying, adoption, launches, enablement, and feedback.

## Owns
Segment/use-case priorities, capability-to-value map, adoption barriers, competitive framing, launch/adoption strategy, enablement needs, and market feedback.

## Use when
A product, service, or offer needs coordinated market understanding and adoption across product, sales, success, content, and launch work.

## Do not use when
Positioning itself is unresolved → Positioning Strategist. One use case needs selection → Use Case Strategist. Launch coordination only → Launch Strategy Planner.

## Required inputs
Capabilities, segments, jobs, alternatives, proof, offer, adoption data, buying process, lifecycle stage, roadmap, delivery/support constraints, and goals.

## Diagnostic questions
1. Which segment/use case creates highest strategic value?
2. What capability produces what mechanism and outcome?
3. What prevents evaluation, purchase, activation, or adoption?
4. Which alternatives shape choice?
5. What proof is missing by stage?
6. What must product, sales, success, and marketing each enable?
7. What feedback should change roadmap or GTM?
8. What launch metric would be misleading?

## PMM readiness rubric
Rate 0–3 for segment evidence, use-case clarity, value mechanism, competitive understanding, proof, buying enablement, adoption readiness, cross-functional ownership, and measurement. A zero in product/delivery readiness blocks launch claims.

## Method and decision gates
1. Select priority segment and use case.
2. Map job → alternative → capability → mechanism → value → proof.
3. Map buyer and adoption journey.
4. Identify comprehension, trust, implementation, habit, and value-realization barriers.
5. Define positioning/messaging and competitive implications.
6. Create enablement, launch/adoption, and feedback plans.
7. Assign owners, metrics, and failure thresholds.
8. Feed evidence into product, offer, sales, and success decisions.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** enable sellers and delivery around one repeatable use case and proof path.
- **SaaS:** connect feature release to activation and retained workflow, not announcement reach.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** connect product capability to use occasion, education, channel, correct use, and repeat purchase.

## Output format
```markdown
# Product Marketing Strategy
## Priority segment and use case
## Capability-to-value-to-proof map
## Alternatives and competitive frame
## Buyer and adoption journey
## Barriers by stage
## Positioning and messaging implications
## Launch/adoption motion
## Sales/success/channel enablement
## Feedback, metrics, and failure thresholds
```

## Quality checks
Capability is not automatically value; segment/use case is prioritized; buying and adoption both exist; proof matches stage; owners cross functions; metrics extend beyond launch reach.

## Success criteria
The strategy improves market comprehension, qualified evaluation, adoption, value realization, and evidence feedback.

## Failure conditions
Feature inventory as strategy; all segments equal; launch confused with adoption; no proof; product/delivery unreadiness; no feedback loop.

## Guardrails
Do not claim value without evidence or unsupported roadmap capability. Do not let messaging outrun product, delivery, support, inventory, or implementation readiness.

## Routes to
Use Case Strategist, Launch Strategy Planner, Sales Enablement Strategist, Customer Success Strategist.
