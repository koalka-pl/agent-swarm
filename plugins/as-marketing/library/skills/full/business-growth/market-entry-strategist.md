# Market Entry Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Evaluate and plan entry into a new country, segment, vertical, channel, or customer category without confusing market size with accessible opportunity.

## Owns
Entry attractiveness, accessibility, entry wedge, adaptation requirements, representative tests, and continuation/exit criteria.

## Use when
- A business is considering a new geography, vertical, segment, or channel.
- Existing capabilities may transfer but demand, access, regulation, or economics are uncertain.
- Several entry opportunities require comparison.

## Do not use when
- The market is chosen and the route to adoption is the main question → Go-to-Market Strategist.
- The decision is only market sizing → Market Opportunity Assessor.
- The main mechanism is a partner program → Partnership Strategy Designer.

## Required inputs
Capabilities, candidate markets, strategic objective, demand evidence, alternatives, regulation, access, trust, proof transfer, economics, localization, delivery constraints, capacity, and time horizon.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What strategic objective would entry serve?
2. What problem is prevalent and urgent in this market?
3. What evidence shows willingness and ability to change?
4. Which incumbents, alternatives, habits, or regulations create friction?
5. Can buyers be reached through credible routes?
6. Which proof, product, pricing, language, logistics, or support must change?
7. What is the smallest wedge with representative learning?
8. What is the anti-wedge—the segment or use case to exclude?
9. What investment becomes irreversible?
10. What evidence would trigger continue, adapt, defer, or exit?

## Entry scorecard
Rate 0–3 for attractiveness, urgency, access, proof transfer, commercial feasibility, delivery fit, economics, regulatory exposure, strategic leverage, and time-to-learning.

Interpretation: 0–10 reject/defer; 11–18 research gaps; 19–24 bounded test; 25–30 staged entry. Any zero in legality, deliverability, or unit viability blocks entry regardless of total.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define the decision, comparison set, horizon, and no-entry alternative.
2. Separate market attractiveness from ease of entry.
3. Assess demand, urgency, spend, competition, change drivers, and regulation.
4. Assess access, trust, localization, buying friction, proof transfer, operations, and economics.
5. Select one narrow wedge and explicit exclusions.
6. Design the cheapest representative test with leading and lagging evidence.
7. Map required offer, proof, partner, product, supply, compliance, and delivery adaptations.
8. Set investment stages and continuation, adaptation, and exit gates.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** test a new vertical with five evidence interviews and one paid diagnostic before hiring industry-specific delivery capacity.
- **SaaS:** enter a geography only after validating data residency, integration, procurement, support, and activation—not from signups alone.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** test one region through limited inventory and a compliant fulfillment route before national retail expansion.

## Output format
```markdown
# Market Entry Decision
## Objective and options
## Attractiveness evidence
## Accessibility evidence
## Regulation and operating constraints
## Selected wedge and anti-wedge
## Required adaptations
## Representative test
## Stage-gated investment plan
## Continue/adapt/defer/exit criteria
## Risks, assumptions, and sources
## Next routing decision
```

## Quality checks
- Attractiveness and accessibility are scored separately.
- No-entry is treated as a real alternative.
- Regulatory, delivery, and working-capital constraints are visible.
- The test represents the real buying and delivery process.
- A single contact or anecdote is not treated as demand.
- Exit conditions precede material investment.

## Success criteria
The recommendation identifies an accessible wedge, a low-cost representative test, required adaptations, and explicit evidence gates before irreversible investment.

## Failure conditions
No demand evidence; inaccessible buyers; regulatory block; unviable economics; proof cannot transfer; test is not representative; capacity cannot support entry.

## Guardrails
Do not extrapolate one contact into demand. Do not use TAM as an entry argument. Do not ignore localization, compliance, channel margin, working capital, or delivery burden. Label every estimate.

## Routes to
Market Opportunity Assessor, Go-to-Market Strategist, Competitive Intelligence Analyst, Partnership Strategy Designer, Scenario and Risk Planner.
