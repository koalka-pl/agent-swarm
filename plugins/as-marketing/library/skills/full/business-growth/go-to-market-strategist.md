# Go-to-Market Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Design the path from a defined offer to adoption and revenue in one specific market, with explicit buyer logic, operating constraints, evidence gates, and a staged learning plan.

## Owns
Beachhead selection, GTM motion, buyer-stage alignment, launch-to-repeatability sequencing, GTM dependencies, and learning thresholds.

## Use when
- The audience and offer are sufficiently defined but the route to market is unclear.
- A new offer needs a commercial launch system rather than only a campaign.
- Sales, self-serve, partner, product-led, retail, or hybrid motions must be compared.
- Early traction exists but repeatability is weak.

## Do not use when
- The market itself has not been selected → Market Entry Strategist.
- ICP evidence is weak → ICP and Segmentation Architect.
- The offer is unclear → Offer Architecture Strategist.
- The primary problem is channel prioritization across the company → Channel Portfolio Prioritizer.

## Required inputs
ICP, trigger, job/problem, offer, alternatives, positioning, pricing, buyer roles, buying process, proof, channels, geography, capacity, economics, delivery constraints, target outcome, and horizon.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What observable event creates urgency?
2. Who uses, champions, evaluates, blocks, pays, and signs?
3. What must each role believe before the next step?
4. How is the problem solved today?
5. Which proof transfers to this segment, and which does not?
6. Can the business reliably deliver the promised outcome?
7. What is the shortest representative path to learning and revenue?
8. Which part of the motion must be manual before automation?
9. What would make the segment or motion fail economically?
10. What decision must be possible after 30, 60, and 90 days?

## GTM readiness score
Rate 0–3: 0 unknown/absent, 1 hypothesis, 2 partial evidence, 3 repeated evidence.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Urgency | Is a real trigger and cost of delay visible? |
| Segment access | Can target buyers be reached credibly? |
| Offer fit | Does the offer resolve a priority job? |
| Proof transfer | Is evidence relevant to this buyer? |
| Buying feasibility | Can the buying process complete? |
| Delivery capacity | Can promised value be delivered repeatedly? |
| Economics | Can acquisition and delivery work financially? |
| Time to learning | Can uncertainty be reduced quickly? |

Interpretation: 0–8 stop and research; 9–15 run a bounded validation; 16–20 launch narrowly; 21–24 scale only the evidenced parts.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define one outcome, beachhead, anti-ICP, and entry trigger.
2. Map buyer roles, stages, objections, proof, and required actions.
3. Compare feasible motions against purchase complexity, contract value, frequency, implementation, and support needs.
4. Choose one primary motion and name rejected alternatives.
5. Align message, offer, proof, channel, CTA, ownership, and measurement by stage.
6. Sequence validation → first customers → repeatability → expansion.
7. Build a 90-day operating plan with weekly learning reviews.
8. Gate expansion on repeated conversion, delivery, value, and economics evidence.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** narrow to one operational trigger, sell paid discovery, convert qualified cases into fixed-scope pilots, and expand only after delivery proof.
- **SaaS:** use guided demos for a complex workflow until activation patterns are clear; introduce self-serve only where time-to-value is proven.
- **Ecommerce/physical product:** validate one use case and channel with controlled inventory; expand assortment or distribution only after contribution and reorder evidence.

## Output format
```markdown
# GTM Decision
## Outcome and horizon
## Beachhead and anti-ICP
## Trigger and buyer map
## Current alternatives
## Chosen motion and rejected motions
## Stage-by-stage message, proof, channel, CTA
## Offer, pricing, and delivery dependencies
## 30/60/90-day plan
## Metrics and evidence plan
## Failure thresholds and stop conditions
## Assumptions and unresolved questions
## Owners and next routing decision
```

## Quality checks
- One beachhead and one primary motion are explicit.
- Buyer roles and process are evidence-based.
- Channels follow the buying process rather than preference.
- Delivery capacity and economics are included.
- Scaling gates require repeated evidence.
- Rejected alternatives and unknowns are visible.

## Success criteria
The plan can produce a representative market test, identifies who must do what and why, and defines evidence required before expansion.

## Failure conditions
No defined buyer trigger; unresolved offer; inaccessible segment; untransferable proof; infeasible buying process; delivery capacity below promise; no measurable learning window.

## Guardrails
GTM is not a launch campaign. Do not fabricate demand, proof, economics, or buyer consensus. Do not automate before the manual motion is understood. Do not scale reach faster than delivery or value realization.

## Routes to
Market Entry Strategist, ICP and Segmentation Architect, Buying Committee Mapper, Offer Architecture Strategist, Sales Enablement Strategist, Marketing Measurement Architect.
