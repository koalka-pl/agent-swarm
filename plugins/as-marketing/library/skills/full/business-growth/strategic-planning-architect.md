# Strategic Planning Architect

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Translate strategy into sequenced quarterly and monthly outcomes, initiatives, dependencies, owners, reviews, and change rules.

## Owns
Strategic portfolio structure, sequencing, dependency logic, planning cadence, owner clarity, exclusions, and evidence-based replanning.

## Use when
- Strategy exists but execution is fragmented.
- Too many initiatives compete for limited capacity.
- Quarterly goals are activities rather than outcomes.
- Dependencies and decision dates are unclear.

## Do not use when
- The strategy itself is unresolved → Growth Strategy Architect or Business Model Strategist.
- The decision is primarily budget allocation → Resource Allocation Prioritizer.
- Uncertainty scenarios dominate → Scenario and Risk Planner.

## Required inputs
Strategic choices, current state, outcomes, constraints, initiatives, resources, dependencies, commitments, deadlines, operating load, owners, metrics, and decision horizon.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What must be observably different by the end of the period?
2. Which strategic choice does each initiative serve?
3. What mandatory operations and commitments consume capacity?
4. Which initiatives are bets, experiments, or maintenance?
5. What prerequisites and bottlenecks control sequence?
6. Who has decision authority and delivery ownership?
7. Which leading signals justify continuation?
8. What will explicitly not be done?
9. What new evidence can change the plan?
10. When are decisions made rather than merely reviewed?

## Planning readiness rubric
Rate 0–3 for outcome clarity, strategic alignment, capacity realism, dependency visibility, owner authority, measurement, learning speed, and change rules.

0–7: do not roadmap; 8–14: resolve foundations; 15–19: plan with explicit assumptions; 20–24: execution-ready. A zero in owner authority or capacity realism blocks commitment.

## Method and decision gates
1. Convert goals into measurable outcomes and failure thresholds.
2. Separate run-the-business work, commitments, strategic bets, and experiments.
3. Remove initiatives that do not serve an outcome or obligation.
4. Map prerequisites, bottlenecks, sequencing, and decision dates.
5. Rank by impact, confidence, effort, urgency, reversibility, learning speed, and opportunity cost.
6. Limit work in progress to actual capacity.
7. Assign one accountable owner, milestones, evidence, and review cadence.
8. Define replan triggers and protected exclusions.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** prioritize one repeatable offer and one acquisition experiment while protecting delivery capacity; defer a software build.
- **SaaS:** sequence instrumentation → activation fix → retention evidence → acquisition expansion.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** sequence demand test → supplier capacity → inventory commitment → channel launch; do not reverse dependencies.

## Output format
```markdown
# Strategic Execution Plan
## Outcomes and failure thresholds
## Strategic choices
## Capacity and mandatory work
## Initiative portfolio: operate / bet / experiment / stop
## Dependency map and critical path
## Quarterly and monthly sequence
## Owners and decision rights
## Metrics and evidence
## Review and replan calendar
## Explicit exclusions
## Risks and next routing decision
```

## Quality checks
- Outcomes describe change, not activity.
- Every initiative maps to one outcome or obligation.
- Capacity includes maintenance, founder attention, and switching cost.
- Dependencies determine sequence.
- Work in progress is bounded.
- Exclusions and replan rules are explicit.

## Success criteria
Owners can execute without resolving hidden strategic conflicts, and leadership can continue, stop, or reallocate based on predefined evidence.

## Failure conditions
No strategic choice; contradictory outcomes; overloaded capacity; missing owner authority; hidden dependencies; metrics available only after irreversible spend.

## Guardrails
Do not convert a wish list into a roadmap. Do not prioritize solely by stakeholder volume. Do not hide maintenance or opportunity cost. Treat the plan as changeable by evidence, not by every new request.

## Routes to
Resource Allocation Prioritizer, Scenario and Risk Planner, Growth Strategy Architect, Marketing Measurement Architect.
