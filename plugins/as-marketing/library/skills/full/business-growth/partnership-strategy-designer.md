# Partnership Strategy Designer

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Identify and design strategic partnerships that create measurable customer, distribution, credibility, capability, data, supply, or operating value.

## Owns
Partner thesis, archetype selection, mutual value exchange, cooperation test, governance, economics, conflict rules, expansion, and exit.

## Use when
- A strategic objective may require another organization’s assets or access.
- Build, buy, or partner alternatives must be compared.
- An existing relationship lacks operating design or measurable value.

## Do not use when
- The task is ongoing partner-channel operations → Partner-Led Growth Strategist.
- The main need is technical data movement → Integration Workflow Designer.
- A new market must first be evaluated → Market Entry Strategist.

## Required inputs
Strategic and customer outcome, owned assets, partner archetypes, shared audience, offer, incentives, proof, integration effort, economics, ownership, data/lead rules, legal constraints, and conflicts.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What customer or strategic outcome cannot be achieved as effectively alone?
2. What specific contribution is required from a partner?
3. What valuable asset can each side credibly provide?
4. Who owns the relationship, execution, and decision rights?
5. Is motivation strong enough to survive after announcement?
6. What conflict, channel, data, brand, or dependency risk exists?
7. What is the smallest joint action that tests behavior?
8. How are economics and attribution handled?
9. What evidence justifies expansion?
10. How can either party exit without harming customers?

## Partnership viability score
Rate 0–3 for customer value, strategic fit, complementary assets, partner motivation, access/trust, operating feasibility, economics, measurement, conflict safety, and reversibility.

0–10 reject; 11–18 research/design; 19–24 pilot; 25–30 expand cautiously. A zero in customer value, motivation, legal safety, or owner capacity blocks launch.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define outcome and compare build, buy, partner, and do-nothing.
2. Select partner archetypes based on required contribution.
3. Map mutual value, costs, incentives, and alternatives.
4. Define target criteria and disqualifiers.
5. Design the smallest joint activation that tests cooperation and customer value.
6. Specify ownership, economics, leads/data, enablement, service, brand, and escalation rules.
7. Measure behavior and outcome, not announcement reach.
8. Gate expansion and define pause/exit conditions.

## Examples
- **B2B service:** co-deliver a narrowly scoped diagnostic with an implementation partner before creating a broad referral agreement.
- **SaaS:** validate one integration’s activation and retained usage before marketplace promotion.
- **Physical product/ecommerce:** test a limited bundle or distributor region with inventory, margin, returns, and customer ownership rules.

## Output format
```markdown

## Strategic and customer outcome
## Build / buy / partner comparison

## Mutual value exchange

## Expansion, pause, and exit conditions
## Assumptions and next routing decision
```

## Quality checks
- Customer value is stronger than logo value.
- Both sides have an observable operating incentive.
- Required contribution and owner are explicit.
- Lead, data, brand, economics, and support rules are defined.
- Pilot measures behavior and value.
- Exit protects customers and data.

## Success criteria
The smallest joint test can reveal whether both parties will act, whether customers benefit, and whether economics and operations support expansion.

## Failure conditions
One-sided value; no accountable owner; incentive depends on goodwill; unavailable integration capacity; unresolved data/legal conflict; no customer outcome; no safe exit.

## Guardrails
A logo exchange is not a partnership. Do not assume access equals motivation or introductions equal pipeline. Do not promise exclusivity, data access, customer ownership, or economics without authority and approval.

## Routes to
Partner-Led Growth Strategist, Integration Workflow Designer, Go-to-Market Strategist, Market Entry Strategist, Business Case Builder.
