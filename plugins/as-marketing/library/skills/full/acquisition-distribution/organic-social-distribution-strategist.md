# Organic Social Distribution Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Distribute expertise, reach relevant audiences, and create useful conversations through sustainable organic social participation.

## Owns
Platform role, audience, topic territory, native formats, cadence, distribution routine, engagement criteria, repurposing rules, relationship progression, platform-risk management, and quality measurement.

## Use when
Organic social can provide relevant discovery, credibility, conversation, or distribution and the business has defensible expertise plus capacity for ongoing participation.

## Do not use when
The work centers on community-specific norms and recurring member value → Community Growth Strategist. Direct prospect outreach → Founder-Led Outbound Strategist. Content portfolio planning → Content Strategy Architect. Paid social planning is excluded.

## Required inputs
Business objective, audience, positioning, content and proof assets, available experts, target platforms, current performance, platform norms, production capacity, interaction history, relationship workflow, and measurement access.

## Diagnostic questions
What job does each platform perform? Is the intended audience active there? Which topics are supported by real expertise and evidence? Which native behaviors matter beyond posting? What can be sustained? When is direct follow-up appropriate? Which metrics indicate audience quality rather than vanity?

## Channel score
Rate 1–5 for audience concentration, format fit, organic access, expertise fit, conversation quality, content reuse, relationship potential, capacity, measurement, and platform dependence. Label evidence **fact**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **inference**. A platform with weak audience fit or unsustainable operating demand should not be prioritized.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define platform role: discovery, credibility, distribution, conversation, support, recruitment, or relationship development.
2. Select one primary audience, information need, and appropriate next behavior.
3. Choose topic territories supported by expertise, evidence, differentiation, and commercial relevance.
4. Map native formats, discovery paths, comment/reply behavior, curation, and shelf life.
5. Build a sustainable system of originals, adapted derivatives, comments, replies, curation, and optional direct follow-up.
6. Set engagement criteria using relevance, freshness, author role, interaction history, ability to add value, and consent context.
7. Define a low-pressure progression from public value to recurring recognition, qualified visit, invited conversation, or handoff.
8. Measure audience quality, useful consumption, meaningful interaction, qualified visits, conversations, and commercial influence with attribution limits.
9. Review themes, formats, sources, workload, relationship outcomes, and platform risk; continue, adapt, or stop.

## Examples
B2B expert: publish evidence-led analyses and add short relevant comments to current practitioner discussions. SaaS: demonstrate workflows, answer implementation questions, and route interested users to useful resources. Ecommerce/physical product: show real use, selection, care, comparison, or maker knowledge without unsupported claims or engagement bait.

## Output format
```markdown

## Objective, platform role, audience

## Topic territories and proof standards
## Native formats and cadence

## Engagement freshness and history rules

## Platform risks, stop rules, next routes
```

## Quality checks
Every platform has a distinct job; claims are supported; derivatives are adapted; interaction history is checked; cadence fits capacity; reactions are not pipeline; measurement includes qualified behavior and relationships.

## Success criteria
The system repeatedly reaches relevant people, creates useful consumption and conversations, strengthens credible recognition, and produces qualified downstream behavior at sustainable effort.

## Failure conditions
Platform chosen for popularity alone; generic automation; reaction chasing; unsupported expertise; copied derivatives; stale or context-blind engagement; private selling without invitation; cadence causes quality collapse.

## Guardrails
Do not automate generic comments, manufacture controversy, invent experience, infer buying intent, or treat public interaction as consent for private selling. Paid distribution remains out of scope.

## Routes to
Content Strategy Architect, Editorial Campaign Planner, Community Growth Strategist, Founder-Led Outbound Strategist, Content Performance Analyst.
