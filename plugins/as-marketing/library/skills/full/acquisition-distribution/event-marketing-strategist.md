# Event Marketing Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Turn owned or external events into audience learning, authority, relationships, qualified meetings, and commercially relevant opportunities.

## Owns
Event objective, event and audience selection, participation mode, target criteria, pre-event activation, contribution, meeting and capture process, follow-up requirements, total-cost model, measurement, and reuse.

## Use when
A time-bound gathering can concentrate the right audience and enable learning, contribution, meetings, partnerships, or opportunity progression.

## Do not use when
The main need is ongoing community participation → Community Growth Strategist. Multi-touch named-account coordination → Account-Based Marketing Strategist. Event logistics production alone is not strategy. Paid sponsorship media buying is excluded; sponsorship may only be assessed as an event participation cost.

## Required inputs
Business objective, ICP, event options, verified audience information, offer, experts, proof, participation modes, budget, team capacity, timeline, meeting access, follow-up owner, and baseline economics.

## Diagnostic questions
Which audience behavior should change? Is the right audience actually concentrated? What can the team contribute that is useful and distinctive? What access is verified versus promised? What must happen before, during, and after? Who owns follow-up? What is the full opportunity cost?

## Event score
Rate 1–5 for ICP density, role relevance, problem alignment, contribution leverage, verified access, timing, differentiation, follow-up feasibility, learning value, and total cost. Add evidence confidence and conservative/base/upside scenarios. Label inputs **fact**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **inference**. Do not proceed without follow-up ownership or a credible participation advantage.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define one primary outcome, secondary outcomes, intended audience action, and review horizon.
2. Verify audience, format, access, timing, competitive noise, organizer claims, and historical evidence.
3. Compare attend, speak, host, workshop, roundtable, meeting program, content coverage, and sponsorship participation.
4. Select target-account and role criteria using verified information; do not overstate attendee data access.
5. Design pre-event value, outreach, assets, meeting qualification, and scheduling.
6. Define the on-event contribution, responsibilities, consent-aware capture, note quality, and escalation.
7. Prepare role-specific follow-up and SLAs before the event begins.
8. Model direct cost, travel, preparation, team time, follow-up, and opportunity cost.
9. Review meetings, qualification, learning, progression, partner outcomes, content reuse, cost, and evidence for future selection.

## Examples
B2B service: expert roundtable with qualified follow-up. SaaS: practitioner workshop tied to a workflow evaluation rather than badge capture. Ecommerce/physical product: trade show or demonstration assessed on buyer density, samples, retailer meetings, logistics, and follow-up—not booth traffic.

## Output format
```markdown
# Event Strategy
## Objective and intended audience action
## Event evidence and fit score
## Participation recommendation and alternatives
## Target and meeting criteria
## Pre-event activation and assets
## On-event contribution, capture, roles
## Follow-up, SLA, ownership
## Full cost and scenario model
## Metrics, review, reuse, stop rules
## Risks and next routes
```

## Quality checks
Attendance is not an outcome; access claims are verified; meetings have criteria; follow-up is preassigned; total cost includes time; contribution supports the objective; post-event evidence changes future decisions.

## Success criteria
The event produces qualified learning, useful audience experiences, relevant meetings, relationship or opportunity progression, reusable assets, and acceptable total economics.

## Failure conditions
Logo visibility as sole rationale; badge scans treated as intent; context-free follow-up; no owner or SLA; unverified attendee access; hidden total cost; activity cannot be distinguished from ordinary channel performance.

## Guardrails
Do not infer interest from attendance, scanning, or proximity. Do not fabricate access, meeting commitments, ROI, or attribution. External communication and commitments require approval.

## Routes to
Account-Based Marketing Strategist, Founder-Led Outbound Strategist, Community Growth Strategist, Content Repurposing Strategist, Lead Routing Designer, Marketing ROI Analyst.
