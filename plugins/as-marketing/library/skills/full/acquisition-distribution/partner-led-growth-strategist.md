# Partner-Led Growth Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Create customer value and qualified growth through complementary organizations, ecosystems, integrations, or shared audiences.

## Owns
Partner model, partner ICP, mutual value, joint motion, activation, enablement requirements, economics assumptions, lead ownership, governance, conflict management, measurement, and exit decisions.

## Use when
Another organization can improve customer outcomes, access, trust, capability, implementation, or distribution and both sides have a concrete operating reason to participate.

## Do not use when
The mechanism is only customer introductions → Referral Program Designer. A single co-hosted event → Event Marketing Strategist. General alliances without a testable customer motion are not ready. Paid affiliate-media execution is excluded.

## Required inputs
ICP, offer, customer journey, ecosystems, current relationships, complementary capabilities, proof, economics, support capacity, legal/compliance constraints, ownership, and conflict risks.

## Diagnostic questions
What customer outcome improves? Why would the partner act now? Which contribution is mutual? Who owns demand, qualification, delivery, data, and support? Can the first value exchange happen cheaply? What creates channel conflict? What proves activation beyond a signed agreement?

## Partner score
Rate 1–5 for customer-value fit, audience overlap, trust, complementary capability, partner motivation, activation ease, economic alignment, governance fit, repeatability, and conflict risk. Add evidence confidence and label inputs **fact**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **inference**. No pilot when customer value or partner motivation is below 3.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define customer and business outcomes plus exclusions.
2. Select model: referral, reseller, implementation, technology, data, content, community, event, association, marketplace, or alliance.
3. Define partner ICP and disqualifiers using value, access, trust, capability, motivation, economics, and support load.
4. Design mutual value and specific contributions; gate out one-sided audience-rental proposals.
5. Specify the joint motion: introduction, co-selling, implementation, integration, bundle, content, event, or distribution.
6. Define qualification, lead registration, consent, ownership, attribution, SLA, customer experience, conflicts, claims, and data boundaries.
7. Create minimum enablement: proposition, proof, qualification, handoff, support, and escalation.
8. Pilot with a small partner set and explicit activation milestone.
9. Continue, redesign, pause, or exit using customer outcomes, sourced quality, progression, cost, and operational burden.

## Examples
B2B service: implementation partnership with complementary specialists. SaaS: integration and co-selling motion tied to a shared workflow. Ecommerce/physical product: retailer, distributor, association, or complementary-brand bundle with explicit margin, fulfillment, claims, and support ownership.

## Quality checks
Customer value comes first; both parties have reasons to act; activation is defined behaviorally; ownership and economics are explicit; first test is bounded; inactive partners exit; support burden is counted.

## Success criteria
Partners repeatedly create measurable customer value, qualified access, implementation leverage, sourced or influenced opportunities, and acceptable economics without unmanaged conflict.

## Failure conditions
Agreement counted as success; one-sided value; unclear ownership; unsupported claims; no activation behavior; economics ignore support; partner volume replaces partner productivity; conflicts remain unresolved.

## Guardrails
Do not assume audience access equals motivation. Do not invent partner commitment, pipeline, or economics. Respect consent, data-sharing limits, commercial disclosure, and applicable incentive rules.
