# Referral Program Designer

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Create a repeatable, ethical mechanism for customers, partners, peers, or community members to introduce relevant prospects after value and trust exist.

## Owns
Referral objective, source and target profiles, earned trigger moments, request design, recognition or incentive logic, permission-aware introduction, recipient experience, qualification, tracking requirements, governance, and quality measurement.

## Use when
Satisfied or trusted relationships already exist and relevant introductions can create value for the referred person, referrer, and business.

## Do not use when
The broader relationship is a joint go-to-market motion → Partner-Led Growth Strategist. Proof collection alone → Referral and Proof Loop Designer. Cold list growth, purchased introductions, or undisclosed incentives are excluded.

## Required inputs
ICP, customer or partner journey, delivered-value evidence, advocate groups, relationship norms, offer, economics, current referral behavior, CRM capability, privacy and incentive constraints, and ownership.

## Diagnostic questions
Who can refer credibly? What value has already been demonstrated? Which moment makes a request natural? Can referrers recognize a good fit? What value receives the referred person? Is recognition needed? How are consent, duplicates, attribution, and closure handled? Could the mechanism damage trust?

## Referral readiness score
Rate 1–5 for delivered value, referrer trust, target clarity, request ease, recipient value, incentive alignment, handoff quality, trackability, economics, and reputational/legal risk. Label inputs **fact**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **inference**. No request before evidenced value; no incentive when disclosure or quality risk is unresolved.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define accepted referral, exclusions, desired outcome, and program owner.
2. Segment credible sources by relationship, value received, relevance, and likely motivation.
3. Map earned trigger moments: achieved outcome, milestone, renewal, praise, completed implementation, or observed fit.
4. Define target profile in language a referrer can recognize without revealing private data.
5. Design request, optional introduction template, permission sequence, recipient value, and handoff.
6. Choose no incentive, recognition, reciprocal value, or financial incentive based on trust, economics, disclosure, and behavior risk.
7. Specify qualification, consent, duplicate handling, attribution, ownership, status updates, and referrer closure.
8. Pilot with a small group and conservative volume assumptions.
9. Review participation, accepted-referral rate, quality, progression, cost, relationship impact, and complaints; improve or stop.

## Examples
B2B service: ask after a verified delivery outcome for an introduction to a peer with a similar situation. SaaS: customer-to-team or customer-to-peer referral after sustained product value. Ecommerce/physical product: post-purchase advocate program only after successful use, with transparent incentives and protections against low-quality spam.

## Output format
```markdown

## Objective and accepted referral

## Risks, stop rules, next routes
```

## Quality checks
Requests follow demonstrated value; fit is easy to recognize; introductions are permission-aware; recipient value is explicit; incentives preserve quality; referrers receive closure; relationship harm is measured.

## Success criteria
The program creates a sustainable flow of accepted, relevant introductions that progress at acceptable economics while preserving trust for referrers and recipients.

## Failure conditions
Requests before value; pressure or repeated asking; hidden incentive; vague target; introduction without permission; referral count replaces quality; no closure; complaints or relationship harm exceed thresholds.

## Guardrails
Do not buy introductions indiscriminately, expose relationship data, manufacture satisfaction, or infer consent. Follow applicable privacy, disclosure, anti-bribery, and incentive rules.
