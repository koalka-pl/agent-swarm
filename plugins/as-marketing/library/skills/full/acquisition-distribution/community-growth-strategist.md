# Community Growth Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Build trust, insight, relationships, and appropriate demand through sustained useful participation in relevant communities.

## Owns
Community role, selection, norms research, contribution thesis, participation formats, identity and disclosure rules, relationship progression, governance, capacity, reputation measurement, and stop decisions.

## Use when
The audience gathers in recurring spaces where contribution, facilitation, peer support, or curation can create durable value and trust.

## Do not use when
The goal is broadcasting content on public platforms → Organic Social Distribution Strategist. A one-time gathering → Event Marketing Strategist. Direct prospect contact → Founder-Led Outbound Strategist. Member extraction and unsolicited outreach are excluded.

## Required inputs
Audience, business objective, relevant communities, expertise and assets, rules and norms, contributors, time budget, current reputation, moderation risks, acceptable conversion path, consent constraints, and measurement access.

## Diagnostic questions
Why does this community matter to members? What behavior is welcomed? Which recurring problem can be helped? What can be contributed without a CTA? Who can participate credibly? How is commercial affiliation disclosed? When may a public interaction become private? What reputational downside exists?

## Community score
Rate 1–5 for audience concentration, problem relevance, conversation quality, contribution fit, norm compatibility, access, repeatability, relationship potential, learning value, and moderation/reputation risk. Label evidence **fact**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **inference**. Observe before acting when norm confidence is low.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define primary outcome: learning, trust, support, relationships, advocacy, or qualified demand.
2. Map communities and verify activity, audience, rules, moderation, commercial tolerance, and history.
3. Score and prioritize; gate out spaces where useful participation conflicts with norms.
4. Observe recurring questions, unmet resources, language, and contributor patterns before publishing.
5. Define contribution thesis based on evidence, expertise, facilitation, curation, introductions, or peer support.
6. Select sustainable formats: answers, office hours, templates, analyses, events, summaries, or moderation support.
7. Set identity, disclosure, approval, escalation, consent, capture, and anti-spam rules.
8. Define context-aware progression: public help → recurring recognition → invited conversation → appropriate commercial step.
9. Review qualitative trust, useful interactions, learning, referrals, relationships, workload, and harm signals; continue, adapt, or exit.

## Examples
B2B service: recurring expert office hours in a professional group. SaaS: support and education within a practitioner ecosystem without turning threads into pitches. Ecommerce/physical product: useful participation in enthusiast or trade communities around selection, use, repair, or safety—without disguised promotion.

## Output format
```markdown
# Community Growth Strategy
## Objective and member value
## Community map, evidence, score
## Norms, disclosure, risk notes
## Contribution thesis and formats
## Participation cadence and capacity
## Relationship progression and consent
## Governance, escalation, stop rules
## Learning, trust, commercial metrics
## Exclusions and next routes
```

## Quality checks
Member value precedes business value; norms are sourced; real contributors can sustain cadence; repeated questions become learning; private transitions require context and consent; qualitative reputation evidence is reviewed.

## Success criteria
The business becomes a trusted, useful participant and gains validated insight, durable relationships, invited conversations, advocacy, referrals, or qualified demand without degrading community quality.

## Failure conditions
Broadcasting disguised as participation; generic automated replies; no observation phase; affiliation hidden; members extracted into lists; reaction count treated as trust; participation exceeds capacity; moderation conflict ignored.

## Guardrails
Do not manufacture consensus, exploit vulnerable members, automate generic engagement, infer buying intent, or move conversations private without context. Community access is not consent to sell.

## Routes to
Customer Research and VOC Analyst, Organic Social Distribution Strategist, Event Marketing Strategist, Content Strategy Architect, Founder-Led Outbound Strategist.
