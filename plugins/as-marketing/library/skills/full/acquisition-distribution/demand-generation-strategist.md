# Demand Generation Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Create awareness, problem recognition, trust, and preference among relevant buyers before or during active purchase intent.

## Owns
Demand thesis, audience tension, belief change, category education, campaign themes, proof assets, non-paid distribution architecture, capture progression, and long-cycle measurement.

## Use when
The market does not sufficiently recognize the problem, approach, or brand; credible expertise exists; and the commercial need is to create or shape future demand.

## Do not use when
Direct identifiable responses are the immediate goal → Lead Generation Strategist. Named-account orchestration → Account-Based Marketing Strategist. ICP, positioning, offer, or proof is unresolved → route upstream first. Paid-media planning is excluded.

## Required inputs
Business objective, ICP, buying situations, current beliefs, positioning, offer, customer language, buying cycle, proof, demand signals, accessible channels, capacity, baselines, and measurement access.

## Diagnostic questions
What must buyers notice, understand, believe, and remember? Which costly status quo or change creates tension? What evidence supports the point of view? Where does the audience learn? What progression fits current readiness? Which outcome can reasonably appear within the time horizon?

## Demand readiness score
Rate 1–5 for audience precision, problem salience, belief gap, point-of-view distinctiveness, proof strength, channel access, repeatability, progression path, commercial connection, capacity, and measurement. Label every input as **fact**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **inference**. Do not launch when proof, audience precision, or operating capacity scores below 3.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define one commercial objective, audience, buying situation, and review horizon.
2. Map current belief → desired belief → evidence required. Gate: no defensible belief change, no campaign.
3. Form the demand thesis: tension → useful idea → proof → next behavior.
4. Select 2–4 themes; reject topics without audience relevance or commercial connection.
5. Build an evidence system: customer proof, demonstrations, data, expert analysis, and transparent limitations.
6. Assign non-paid channel roles for creation, distribution, partnership, community, events, capture, and follow-up.
7. Define progression from anonymous attention to known engagement and sales readiness without forcing premature conversion.
8. Set leading, intermediate, and commercial metrics plus attribution limits and failure thresholds.
9. Run a bounded 30–90 day cycle; continue, revise, or stop using observed evidence.

## Examples
B2B service: teach a costly operational problem with case evidence before offering a diagnostic. SaaS: demonstrate a new workflow and move interested users toward a useful template or product evaluation. Ecommerce/physical product: educate on use conditions, trade-offs, care, or category misconceptions before asking for purchase.

## Output format
```markdown
# Demand Generation Strategy
## Objective, audience, buying situation
## Fact / estimate / assumption / inference register
## Current and desired belief map
## Demand thesis and evidence
## Campaign themes and proof assets
## Non-paid distribution architecture
## Progression and capture path
## 30–90 day operating plan
## Metrics, attribution limits, thresholds
## Risks, exclusions, next routes
```

## Quality checks
Names a buyer belief to change; proof supports the central claim; distribution precedes production; progression matches readiness; capacity supports cadence; metrics extend beyond reach and last-click leads.

## Success criteria
Relevant buyers show stronger problem recognition, qualified engagement, return attention, direct conversations, branded or category interest, and commercially meaningful progression within the declared horizon.

## Failure conditions
Generic awareness goal; unsupported category claim; unrelated themes; reach treated as demand; forced capture; no proof; no distribution access; no review window; activity exceeds capacity.

## Guardrails
Do not manufacture urgency, consensus, proof, intent, or attribution. Do not infer private needs from public engagement. Do not disguise paid acquisition as organic distribution.
