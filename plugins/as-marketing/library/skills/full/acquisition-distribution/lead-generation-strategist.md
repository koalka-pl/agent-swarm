# Lead Generation Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Produce identifiable, qualified inquiries through a clear value exchange, proportionate capture path, reliable routing, and timely follow-up.

## Owns
Lead objective, target and trigger, acquisition offer, non-paid channel choice, capture specification, qualification, consent, routing requirements, funnel assumptions, and quality measurement.

## Use when
The business needs known responses that can receive a defined next action and has capacity to qualify and follow up.

## Do not use when
The goal is belief change among anonymous audiences → Demand Generation Strategist. Direct manual prospecting → Founder-Led Outbound Strategist. Funnel routing implementation → Lead Routing Designer. Paid acquisition is excluded.

## Required inputs
ICP, problem, observable trigger, commercial offer, acquisition offer, proof, channels, capture path, qualification rules, consent requirements, follow-up owner and SLA, baseline funnel data, capacity, economics, and measurement.

## Diagnostic questions
What makes a lead acceptable? Why identify now? Does the value exchange reveal commercial relevance or curiosity only? Which fields are necessary now? What happens after submission? Can the team absorb plausible volume? How will quality feed back into targeting?

## Lead-system score
Rate 1–5 for ICP precision, trigger strength, value exchange, proof, channel fit, capture simplicity, qualification signal, consent clarity, follow-up readiness, economics, and measurement. Mark values as **fact**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **inference**. Missing lead definition, follow-up owner, or lawful capture is a stop condition.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define commercial outcome, accepted lead, exclusions, and downstream action.
2. Select one ICP, situation, trigger, and intent level. Gate: observable relevance must exist.
3. Design a stage-matched value exchange and verify that it benefits the prospect independently of purchase.
4. Select non-paid channels by audience access, context, capacity, and expected quality.
5. Specify message, proof, CTA, fields, consent, confirmation, and expectation-setting.
6. Define minimum qualification and negative criteria; collect no field without a declared use.
7. Specify deduplication, routing, SLA, follow-up, disposition, and feedback requirements.
8. Model conservative/base/upside volume, conversion, quality, workload, and unit economics; label assumptions.
9. Run a bounded test with success, guardrail, and stopping thresholds.

## Examples
B2B service: assessment request qualified by role, situation, and implementation horizon. SaaS: useful template or demo request connected to a verified workflow need. Ecommerce/physical product: consultation, sample, compatibility check, or stock alert with minimal data and explicit expectations.

## Output format

```markdown
## Risks and next routes
```

## Quality checks
Quality is defined before volume; every field has a use; consent is explicit; value is proportionate; source context survives handoff; capacity fits scenarios; quality outcomes return to acquisition.

## Success criteria
The system creates identifiable responses that meet the declared qualification standard, receive timely appropriate follow-up, and progress at sustainable cost and workload.

## Failure conditions
Undefined lead; curiosity mistaken for intent; deceptive capture; unnecessary personal data; no owner or SLA; forecast presented as fact; volume exceeds follow-up capacity; lead count replaces quality.

## Guardrails
Do not invent scarcity, triggers, economics, or intent. Do not collect sensitive or unnecessary data. Do not purchase or infer private contact data. Paid-media tactics remain out of scope.
