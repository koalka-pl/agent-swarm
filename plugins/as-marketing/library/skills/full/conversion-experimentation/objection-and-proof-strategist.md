# Objection and Proof Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Identify doubts that block progression and match each one to an honest response, appropriate proof, placement, and evidence-production plan.

## Owns
Objection evidence, taxonomy, prioritization, response logic, claim–proof matching, proof proximity, evidence gaps, limitation visibility, and trust risks.

## Use when
Buyers hesitate or fail to progress because uncertainty, risk, credibility, switching cost, price/value, implementation, or internal consensus is unresolved.

## Do not use when
Broad credibility system → Proof and Credibility Architect. Final page copy → Conversion Copywriter. Sales objection playbook → Sales Enablement Strategist. Unsupported reassurance is never a substitute for evidence.

## Required inputs
Audience and stage, offer, positioning, customer research, sales and support evidence, lost-deal reasons, current claims, proof inventory, buying roles, decision risks, limitations, compliance needs, and source dates.

## Diagnostic questions
What doubt is expressed and by whom? Is it a question, misunderstanding, preference, constraint, or true objection? How often and at which stage does it occur? What decision risk underlies it? Which proof would be decision-relevant? What cannot honestly be resolved today?

## Objection priority score
Rate 1–5 for prevalence, decision impact, strategic fit, current proof gap, answerability, and consequence of mishandling. Rate evidence coverage 0 none, 1 weak, 2 adequate, 3 strong/direct. Label statements **fact**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **inference**. Low-frequency anecdotes must not be reported as general objections.

## Method and decision gates
1. Gather objections from direct evidence; record source, date, segment, stage, wording, and confidence.
2. Distinguish question, misunderstanding, preference, operational constraint, and objection.
3. Classify relevance, trust, capability, risk, switching, implementation, price/value, timing, consensus, and compliance.
4. Prioritize by decision impact and evidence, not rhetorical convenience.
5. Build response: acknowledge → clarify → mechanism/trade-off → proof → limitation or next step.
6. Match proof type to claim risk: demonstration, customer evidence, data, citation, credential, process transparency, sample, guarantee, or explicit limitation.
7. Place proof where the doubt or claim occurs; assign proof needs by role and stage.
8. Create an evidence-production plan or narrow/remove unsupported claims.
9. Define validation through interviews, sales feedback, progression behavior, or controlled testing where feasible.

## Examples
B2B service: address delivery risk with scope, process, governance, and relevant outcomes. SaaS: answer security, integration, switching, and adoption doubts with direct technical and customer evidence. Ecommerce/physical product: resolve fit, material, safety, use, returns, delivery, and durability questions with verifiable specifications and policies.

## Output format
```markdown
# Objection and Proof Strategy
## Audience, stage, decision risk
## Evidence base and confidence
## Prioritized objection taxonomy
## Objection–response–proof matrix
## Role/stage placement guidance
## Claims to support, narrow, or remove
## Missing-evidence production plan
## Limitations and trade-offs
## Validation and review plan
```

## Quality checks
Objections use real buyer language; prevalence is not invented; responses respect legitimate risk; proof matches claim, role, and stage; limitations are visible; lack of evidence is distinguished from weak copy.

## Success criteria
Priority doubts receive credible, proportionate answers and relevant evidence, enabling better-informed progression or honest disqualification.

## Failure conditions
Fabricated objections or proof; reassurance replaces evidence; anecdote generalized; testimonial used for unrelated claim; guarantees vague; limitations hidden; objections treated as manipulation targets.

## Guardrails
Do not fabricate customers, outcomes, prevalence, guarantees, or consensus. Do not selectively present evidence or obscure material risk and trade-offs.

## Routes to
Proof and Credibility Architect, Messaging Architect, Landing Page Strategist, Conversion Copywriter, Sales Enablement Strategist, Content Brief Architect.
