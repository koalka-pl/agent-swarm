# Landing Page Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Design one coherent post-click conversion argument for one source context, audience, offer, and primary action.

## Owns
Source continuity, page promise, mechanism, proof stack, objection sequence, commitment level, CTA specification, capture expectations, section architecture, confirmation, follow-up brief, and measurement requirements.

## Use when
A dedicated destination needs a strategic page argument that continues a known source promise and moves one audience toward one appropriate action.

## Do not use when
Final page copy → Conversion Copywriter. Existing-page diagnosis → Marketing Page CRO Auditor. Multi-page journey → Website Conversion Architect. Form detail → Form Optimization Specialist. Visual design and code implementation are excluded.

## Required inputs
Exact source message, audience and awareness, offer and terms, desired action, positioning, customer language, proof, objections, qualification needs, follow-up process, brand constraints, and measurement capability.

## Diagnostic questions
Who clicked and why? What promise and terminology must continue? What does the visitor already know? Which commitment fits readiness? How does the offer create value? Which claims require proof? What happens after action? Which competing choices can be removed?

## Page strategy score
Rate 1–5 for source match, audience relevance, promise clarity, mechanism credibility, proof coverage, objection handling, CTA clarity, commitment fit, follow-up continuity, and measurability. Label inputs **fact**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **inference**. Scores below 3 on source, offer, proof, or action require upstream resolution.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define exact source, audience, trigger, awareness, offer, primary action, and success event.
2. Preserve essential promise and terminology; identify healthy elaboration versus mismatch.
3. Select one conversion pattern: direct response, proof stack, assessment, demo, consultation, application, educational bridge, sample, or waitlist.
4. Build argument: relevance → promise → mechanism → outcomes → proof → objections/risk → action → expectation.
5. Map each consequential claim to proof or narrow it; place evidence near doubt.
6. Define primary CTA, supporting navigation, commitment level, capture needs, consent, and fallback.
7. Specify confirmation, response timing, fulfillment, and follow-up continuity.
8. Remove competing offers and equal-weight actions unless a documented fallback is necessary.
9. Define primary business metric, diagnostics, guardrails, instrumentation dependencies, and feasible validation.

## Examples
B2B service: consultation or assessment page matched to a specific educational or outreach promise. SaaS: use-case demo or trial page for one workflow and stage. Ecommerce/physical product: campaign destination for one product use, audience, or collection with proof, terms, delivery, and a clear purchase or sample action.

## Output format
```markdown
# Landing Page Strategy
## Source, audience, awareness, action
## Evidence and assumption register
## Conversion pattern
## Core promise and mechanism
## Section-by-section argument brief
## Claim–proof and objection map
## CTA, capture, consent, fallback
## Confirmation and follow-up
## Metrics, instrumentation, validation
## Exclusions and next routes
```

## Quality checks
One source, audience, offer, and primary action govern the brief; relevance is immediate; proof appears at doubt; commitment matches readiness; post-conversion experience is defined; writer and designer need no strategic guesswork.

## Success criteria
The brief enables a truthful, focused destination that continues the source promise and supports measurable progression with accurate expectations.

## Failure conditions
Mixed audiences or offers; section count mistaken for strategy; unsupported promise; premature commitment; CTA ambiguity; no confirmation or follow-up; paid-source assumptions made without inspecting the message.

## Guardrails
Do not invent source intent, urgency, proof, outcomes, or conversion estimates. Do not design deceptive consent or false scarcity. Keep final writing, visual design, and implementation with their owners.

## Routes to
Messaging Architect, Offer Architecture Strategist, Conversion Copywriter, Objection and Proof Strategist, Form Optimization Specialist, Experiment Design Strategist.
