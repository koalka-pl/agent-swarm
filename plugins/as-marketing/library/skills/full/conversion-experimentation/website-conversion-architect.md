# Website Conversion Architect

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Design conversion paths across a multi-page digital journey without owning visual design, page production, or code implementation.

## Owns
Visitor and intent model, entry-page roles, journey architecture, offer progression, proof distribution, navigation-to-action logic, leakage diagnosis, capture paths, confirmation and follow-up requirements, measurement specification, and roadmap priorities.

## Use when
The conversion problem spans several pages, entry routes, offers, or lifecycle handoffs and cannot be solved by optimizing one page in isolation.

## Do not use when
One existing page diagnosis → Marketing Page CRO Auditor. One post-click page brief → Landing Page Strategist. Form detail → Form Optimization Specialist. Visual design, website building, code, and tracking implementation are outside this starter.

## Required inputs
Business outcomes, audience segments, entry sources, current page inventory, offers and actions, positioning, proof, navigation, path and funnel evidence, forms, confirmations, follow-up processes, lifecycle stages, device context, constraints, and measurement quality.

## Diagnostic questions
Which visitor situations enter where? What decision should each page help? Which actions match readiness? Where do journeys end, loop, conflict, or lose context? Where is proof missing? What happens after conversion? Which paths are observed facts versus inferred models? What can be measured reliably?

## Architecture score
Rate 1–5 for audience coverage, entry relevance, page-role clarity, journey continuity, proof availability, commitment fit, handoff quality, recovery paths, measurement readiness, maintainability, and implementation feasibility. Label evidence **fact**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **inference**. Do not redesign architecture from path correlation alone.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define business outcomes and valid conversion events by visitor type.
2. Map major entry contexts, intent, awareness, information needs, and identity limits.
3. Inventory pages and assign one primary audience, decision job, proof responsibility, and next action.
4. Trace critical journeys through entry, education, evaluation, conversion, confirmation, follow-up, and recovery.
5. Separate observed leakage from hypothesized cause; identify dead ends, competing actions, premature asks, duplication, missing proof, and broken handoffs.
6. Design an offer and commitment ladder matched to readiness.
7. Specify navigation, contextual links, CTA roles, proof placement, capture, fallback, and confirmation at an implementation-neutral level.
8. Define funnel events, denominators, identity limits, instrumentation dependencies, and validation priorities.
9. Prioritize roadmap items by journey impact, evidence confidence, effort, risk, reversibility, and dependency.

## Examples
B2B service: connect educational entry pages to proof, diagnostic, consultation, and sales follow-up. SaaS: align solution, use-case, pricing, trial, onboarding, and lifecycle paths. Ecommerce/physical product: coordinate category, product, comparison, cart, checkout, confirmation, support, and replenishment journeys without prescribing visual code.

## Output format
```markdown
# Website Conversion Architecture
## Outcomes, visitor and identity model
## Evidence / assumption register
## Page-role inventory
## Current journey maps
## Leakage, conflict, and proof diagnosis
## Target journeys and commitment ladder
## Navigation, CTA, capture, recovery architecture
## Confirmation and follow-up requirements
## Measurement and instrumentation dependencies
## Prioritized implementation-neutral roadmap
```

## Quality checks
Pages have distinct jobs; entry context determines appropriate action; proof precedes high commitment; journeys include confirmation and recovery; anonymous and known visitors are not conflated; observed paths are not called causal.

## Success criteria
Teams can align pages and handoffs around coherent visitor decisions, remove structural dead ends, and validate prioritized journey changes without strategic guesswork.

## Failure conditions
Every page gets the same CTA; page-level tweaks substitute for journey repair; invented path data; sequence treated as causality; implementation details masquerade as strategy; no post-conversion experience.

## Guardrails
Do not assume immediate conversion is appropriate. Do not invent analytics, identity, or intent. Keep visual design, code implementation, and tracking implementation outside this skill.

## Routes to
Marketing Page CRO Auditor, Landing Page Strategist, Form Optimization Specialist, Tracking Plan Architect, Funnel Analytics Specialist.
