# Marketing Page CRO Auditor

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Diagnose why an existing marketing page may underperform and prioritize evidence-backed conversion hypotheses without claiming untested improvement.

## Owns
Page role definition, traffic context, conversion-argument diagnosis, friction findings, evidence grading, hypothesis backlog, prioritization, and validation brief.

## Use when
An existing marketing page has a defined business action but performance, clarity, relevance, trust, or friction may be limiting progression and the cause is uncertain.

## Do not use when
Multi-page journey architecture → Website Conversion Architect. New post-click strategy → Landing Page Strategist. Final copy → Conversion Copywriter. Visual design, code implementation, and paid-campaign optimization are excluded.

## Required inputs
Page or captured content, audience, exact source messages, offer, desired action, denominator and funnel data, qualitative feedback, proof, objections, device mix, accessibility constraints, tracking quality, and change history.

## Diagnostic questions
What job does the page perform? Who arrives and from where? Is relevance obvious? Does the source promise continue? Is one action primary? Are claims supported where doubt occurs? Is friction qualifying or accidental? What evidence supports each diagnosis? Can outcomes be measured reliably?

## CRO evidence score
Rate 1–5 for message match, clarity, relevance, value specificity, mechanism, proof, objection coverage, CTA, friction, expectation setting, accessibility, and measurement readiness. For every finding label **observation**, **interpretation**, **hypothesis**, **proposed change**, and **validation**; separately mark source evidence as fact, estimate, assumption, or inference.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define page role, audience, traffic context, conversion event, denominator, and post-action experience.
2. Inspect exact major source messages; do not infer them from channel labels.
3. Trace the argument: relevance → problem → promise → mechanism → outcome → proof → objection/risk → CTA → expectation.
4. Evaluate hierarchy, specificity, credibility, cognitive load, interaction friction, accessibility, and mobile context.
5. Review behavioral, funnel, qualitative, and technical evidence with sampling limitations.
6. Separate observed issue from causal hypothesis and proposed remedy.
7. Prioritize by expected business impact, evidence confidence, effort, risk, reversibility, and dependency.
8. Recommend the smallest change that can address or test the evidence; define metrics, guardrails, and decision method.
9. Explicitly list changes not recommended and unknowns requiring research.

## Examples
B2B service: audit a consultation page with unclear qualification and weak proof. SaaS: diagnose a pricing or demo page by traffic source and stage. Ecommerce/physical product: review product or category argument, specifications, delivery, returns, trust, and action friction without assuming checkout causality.

## Output format
```markdown
# Marketing Page CRO Audit
## Page role, audience, source, action
## Data and instrumentation limits
## Conversion-argument assessment
## Findings: observation → interpretation → hypothesis
## Prioritized issue table
## Exact strategic change briefs
## Validation method, metrics, guardrails
## Not recommended and why
## Dependencies and next routes
```

## Quality checks
Actual traffic context is used; sections are judged as one argument; claims map to proof; recommendations are not generic best practices; business outcomes, not proxy clicks alone, define priority; causal language matches evidence.

## Success criteria
The audit produces a small, evidence-ranked set of actionable hypotheses with clear owners and validation methods, reducing uncertainty before implementation.

## Failure conditions
Invented benchmarks or motivations; full redesign by default; heatmaps treated as representative truth; correlation called cause; issue list without prioritization; change prescribed without measurement.

## Guardrails
Do not invent data or results, guarantee uplift, or claim an untested fix worked. Do not optimize deceptive friction or dark patterns. Keep implementation outside this skill.
