# Proposal and Business Case Writer

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Translate verified discovery into a buyer-ready scope, commercial proposal, and optional internal decision case.

## Owns
Proposal narrative, customer situation summary, outcome, scope, approach, milestones, deliverables, acceptance, responsibilities, assumptions, exclusions, value logic, evidence, risk, decision request, and approval checklist.

## Use when
Discovery, offer, scope, delivery feasibility, price, and decision process are sufficiently confirmed to produce a commercial document.

## Do not use when
Business-case analysis without proposal → Business Case Builder. Offer or price design → Offer Architecture Strategist / Pricing and Packaging Strategist. Scope risk → Scope and Delivery Risk Analyst. Contract drafting or legal advice is excluded.

## Required inputs
Verified discovery record, desired outcome, baseline, stakeholders, buying criteria, approved scope, deliverables, assumptions, exclusions, success/acceptance, risks, timeline, responsibilities, price, terms, proof, and decision path.

## Diagnostic questions
Which customer statements are confirmed? What decision is being requested? What outcome and scope are approved? Which dependencies remain outside supplier control? How is value calculated? Which terms are fixed? Can delivery support every commitment? What must the buyer share internally?

## Proposal readiness score
Rate 0–2 for problem confirmation, outcome clarity, scope readiness, stakeholder coverage, proof, acceptance criteria, delivery feasibility, commercial approval, value evidence, and decision path. Label content **fact**, **customer-stated**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **open decision**. No proposal when scope, price approval, or delivery feasibility is unresolved.

## Method and decision gates
1. Restate customer situation and decision using confirmed evidence and attributed language.
2. Define outcome, scope, approach, milestones, deliverables, acceptance, responsibilities, and governance.
3. Make assumptions, dependencies, exclusions, data/access needs, and change conditions explicit.
4. Connect price to approved scope and value logic; show ranges, formulas, and uncertainty without fabricated ROI.
5. Build internal case: why consider change, alternatives including no action, expected value range, cost/risk, implementation needs, and decision requested.
6. Tailor proof and concerns to economic, operational, technical, user, and approval roles.
7. Preserve fixed legal terms exactly; state validity, approval path, next step, and unresolved decisions.
8. Review factual accuracy, commercial consistency, delivery capacity, tone, and confidentiality.

## Examples
B2B service: phased implementation proposal with shared responsibilities and value scenarios. SaaS: commercial evaluation with licenses, onboarding, integration, adoption, and success terms. Ecommerce/physical product B2B: wholesale/distribution proposal covering assortment, volume assumptions, margin, fulfillment, returns, compliance, and review points.

## Output format
```markdown
# Proposal and Business Case
## Executive decision summary
## Confirmed situation and desired outcome
## Scope, approach, milestones, deliverables
## Acceptance and success criteria
## Responsibilities, dependencies, exclusions
## Evidence and stakeholder relevance
## Commercials and value assumptions
## Risks, alternatives, open decisions
## Terms, validity, approvals, next step
## Claim/evidence and review checklist
```

## Quality checks
No unconfirmed scope appears as fact; value ranges expose assumptions; exclusions are visible; proof is relevant; commitments match delivery; next decision is clear; fixed legal text is unchanged.

## Success criteria
The buyer can evaluate fit, value, risk, responsibilities, commercial terms, and next decision while the delivery team can honor the proposed commitments.

## Failure conditions
Fabricated ROI, budget, urgency, stakeholder support, or customer language; vague scope; hidden dependency; price inconsistent with approval; proposal used to discover basic needs; legal wording paraphrased.

## Guardrails
Do not invent value, commitments, or authority. Preserve contractual text exactly and route legal review appropriately. External commercial commitment requires approval.

## Routes to
Offer Architecture Strategist, Scope and Delivery Risk Analyst, Pricing and Packaging Strategist, Buying Committee Mapper, Business Case Builder.
