# Sales Enablement Strategist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Create the minimum proof, tools, guidance, and feedback system needed to help buyers and sellers progress real decisions.

## Owns
Stage-specific buyer questions, stakeholder needs, asset inventory, proof coverage, gap prioritization, usage guidance, access, ownership, versioning, retirement, adoption, and effectiveness measurement.

## Use when
Sales repeatedly encounters information, proof, process, or stakeholder gaps that can be resolved through reusable decision support.

## Do not use when
Broad content strategy → Content Strategy Architect. Final proposal → Proposal and Business Case Writer. Objection evidence mapping → Objection and Proof Strategist. More collateral is not the default answer.

## Required inputs
Sales stages, buying roles, offer, positioning, objections, proof inventory, discovery/call evidence, loss reasons, seller workflow, existing collateral, usage data, ownership, and compliance requirements.

## Diagnostic questions
Which buyer decision is blocked? For which role and stage? Is the gap information, proof, confidence, process, or seller skill? What already exists? Can sellers find and use it? Does the buyer need an asset or a conversation? Who maintains truth?

## Enablement priority score
Rate 1–5 for frequency, stage impact, revenue/customer risk, proof deficit, stakeholder breadth, reuse potential, adoption feasibility, production effort, and staleness risk. Label inputs **fact**, **estimate**, **assumption**, or **inference**. Low-use assets should be repaired or retired before new production.

## Method and decision gates
1. Map buyer decisions, stakeholders, evidence needs, and seller actions by stage.
2. Gather recurring questions, objections, delays, losses, and handoff failures from direct evidence.
3. Audit assets for accuracy, relevance, duplication, findability, adoption, and expiration.
4. Identify whether the remedy is an asset, proof, training, process change, product input, or upstream strategy.
5. Prioritize the smallest reusable intervention with a named decision job.
6. Brief content, tool, checklist, calculator, demo, case evidence, or mutual plan with proof requirements and exclusions.
7. Define user, timing, channel, preparation, follow-up, owner, version, access, and retirement.
8. Measure adoption, buyer use, stage progression, cycle effects, and qualitative decision value without claiming causality from correlation.

## Examples
B2B service: discovery guide, relevant case proof, scope checklist, and mutual action plan. SaaS: role-specific demo path, security evidence, implementation guide, and champion kit. Ecommerce/physical product B2B: assortment deck, specifications, compliance, margin/fulfillment tool, and retailer onboarding guide.

## Output format
```markdown
# Sales Enablement Strategy
## Buyer decisions and stakeholder needs
## Evidence base and recurring gaps
## Asset inventory: keep / fix / retire
## Prioritized enablement backlog
## Asset/tool briefs and proof needs
## Stage, role, usage guidance
## Ownership, versioning, access, retirement
## Adoption and outcome measurement
## Risks and next routes
```

## Quality checks
Every intervention serves a named buyer decision; content is current and findable; usage is explicit; buyer use matters; unsupported claims are blocked; retirement is part of governance.

## Success criteria
Sellers and buyers can access accurate decision support at the right stage, reducing avoidable confusion, repeated work, proof gaps, and handoff friction.

## Failure conditions
Asset count as success; seller-only perspective; duplicate collateral; no owner/version; unsupported proof; training used to mask a broken offer or process; unused assets remain active.

## Guardrails
Do not fabricate claims, customers, objections, or effectiveness. Do not create materials that enable pressure, deception, or disclosure of confidential customer information.

## Routes to
Buying Committee Mapper, Objection and Proof Strategist, Proposal and Business Case Writer, Content Brief Architect.
