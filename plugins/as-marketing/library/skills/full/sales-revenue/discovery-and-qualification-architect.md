# Discovery and Qualification Architect

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maturity:** full skill

## Purpose
Structure mutual discovery so buyer and seller understand the problem, impact, constraints, fit, buying process, and appropriate next decision.

## Owns
Discovery objectives, conversation structure, question bank, qualification dimensions, evidence standards, mutual commitments, stage exit, disqualification, summary format, and CRM requirements.

## Use when
A business needs repeatable discovery and qualification that improves decision quality without turning conversations into scripted interrogation.

## Do not use when
Initial targeting/outreach → Founder-Led Outbound Strategist. Pipeline stages → Pipeline Process Designer. Proposal drafting → Proposal and Business Case Writer. Offer, pricing, or final solution design must route to their owners.

## Required inputs
ICP, offer, known jobs and triggers, sales stage, buying roles, delivery constraints, historical wins/losses, qualification patterns, CRM, compliance constraints, and desired decision.

## Diagnostic questions
What decision must discovery support? Which customer evidence is essential? What fatal constraints exist? Who experiences, owns, funds, approves, implements, and uses the solution? What is confirmed, interpreted, assumed, or unknown? Which mutual next step demonstrates real progress?

## Qualification score
Rate 0–2 for problem evidence, impact, urgency/change trigger, offer fit, stakeholder access, success clarity, implementation feasibility, commercial path, decision process, and mutual commitment. Label statements **fact**, **buyer interpretation**, **seller assumption**, or **unknown**. No total overrides fatal legal, delivery, ethical, or fit constraints.

## Method and decision gates
1. Define the stage decision and minimum evidence required.
2. Cover situation, problem, current approach, consequences, desired outcome, success measure, technical/data conditions, stakeholders, process, commercial conditions, timing, and risks.
3. Ask open questions, follow evidence, summarize, and invite correction; do not force a framework mechanically.
4. Separate observed facts, buyer interpretations, seller hypotheses, and unknowns.
5. Assess fit, readiness, risk, value logic, implementation, and mutual commitment independently.
6. Identify the buying committee and missing access without inventing authority or budget.
7. Choose advance, validate, nurture, refer, disqualify, or stop; define evidence for the next step.
8. Save only decision-relevant fields, sources, dates, owners, and commitments.

## Examples
B2B service: qualify operational impact, sponsor, delivery access, and decision path. SaaS: explore workflow, users, integration, security, adoption, and evaluation process. Ecommerce/physical product B2B: qualify retailer/distributor demand, assortment, margin, logistics, compliance, and buyer timeline.

## Output format
```markdown
# Discovery and Qualification System
## Decision and stage scope
## Discovery conversation structure
## Question bank by dimension
## Evidence and uncertainty labels
## Qualification score and fatal constraints
## Advance / validate / nurture / disqualify logic
## Mutual commitments and stage exit
## Summary and CRM schema
## Quality review and next routes
```

## Quality checks
Questions support a decision; buyer language is preserved; assumptions stay visible; qualification includes delivery fit and mutual commitment; disqualification is explicit; records are concise and useful.

## Success criteria
Teams reach clearer mutual decisions, reduce false-positive opportunities, identify missing evidence early, and progress qualified deals with explicit commitments.

## Failure conditions
Interrogation script; polite interest called qualification; company size used as budget evidence; urgency manufactured; score hides fatal constraint; next step lacks owner or purpose.

## Guardrails
Do not invent budget, authority, need, urgency, or timeline. Do not pressure disclosure of unnecessary sensitive information. Discovery should improve buyer as well as seller clarity.

## Routes to
Buying Committee Mapper, Proposal and Business Case Writer, Pipeline Process Designer, Paid Pilot Designer.
